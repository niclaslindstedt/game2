# Getting started

## Playing

Open [game2.niclaslindstedt.se](https://game2.niclaslindstedt.se/). The app opens on an attract screen: the studio card while the game loads, then the title under two crossed finish flags and **press any key to start** (tap, on a phone). It waits for you — the press is also what lets the browser turn the sound on. Behind it the game is already running, and clearing the card drops you on the **main menu**, which is not a still: a bot is driving a real stage behind it, seen from a drone, and it keeps going while you decide. Four ways in:

- **Campaign** — a location (Taiga, for now) and its six stages, each one unlocked by getting on the **podium** of the one before it, and the location after it unlocked by finishing **top of its table**. The campaign IS its championship: one board, one set of points, nothing stacked on top. Locked stages wear a grey border and a padlock; open ones wear green. The ladder climbs in both length and difficulty, from a 1-minute forest road to seven minutes of hairpins and jumps in a night storm — and then changes discipline: the last two are **circuits**, three laps of a closed lap that comes back to its own start line, where you get to learn the road and the clock is the whole opponent.

  You are not out there alone. Every campaign stage is run against **fourteen other crews**, leaving the start control ten seconds apart the way a real rally does, and you are the last car on the road. The stage opens on the camera circling the start control while the crew in front of you — stood on the line beside you, not on top of you — gets its green and drives away; the ten seconds you are watching are the ten seconds of head start they have. Any pedal cuts it short, and the camera flies quickly to your driving view rather than jumping there. Everybody ahead is genuinely up the road, so there is nothing to overtake for position, only times to beat; but they ARE out there, in their own cars and their own colours, and if you catch one you can touch them. So can they touch you. Your **position** rides in the top right, beside the map, and it moves at every split board: your place is every car already through that board, plus you. The split itself names the crew you are measured against — the leader through it. **Finish in the top three and the stage is cleared**; finish outside it and the card at the line says TOO SLOW, and the stage is still yours to run again. **RIVALS** on the stage list picks how good the field is — EASY, MEDIUM or HARD. Each stage box remembers your best finishing position at the setting you are looking at, beside your best time.

  And every stage is worth **points**: three for the win, two for second, one for third, nothing below that — to you and to the fourteen crews alike. Each stage box carries what it is currently paying you, so the ladder and the table are the same board. At the line the card says what the place paid you and where the location now stands, and **FULL RESULTS** opens the whole sheet: all fifteen crews in finishing order, their times, what the stage paid each of them, and what they have for the location. **Run a stage as many times as you like** — for a better place, a better time and better points; the board keeps whichever attempt paid you MORE, so a lap driven for fun can never cost you a title.

  Those points are the whole campaign, at both scales: a stage opens the one after it by paying you something (a podium), and a location opens the next COUNTRY by being driven all the way through with you top of its table — which in practice means winning stages rather than scraping onto podiums. The **STANDINGS** panel under the stage list shows where you stand, **CONTINUE** takes you to the next stage to drive (forward while there are stages you have not seen, then back to the first one you have not WON), **FULL TABLE** shows all fifteen crews, and **RESET LOCATION** tears the location's points up so you can drive it again from the start — the stages stay open in the time trial; the points, and the rungs they opened, go.

- **Time trial** — the same stages behind a gate of its own, with your best time on each: a stage opens here once you have driven it to the FINISH LINE in the campaign — podium or not — because a time is something you chase on a road you have already seen the end of, and a stage you crossed the line on in ninth is exactly the road you now want the clock on. Nobody else is entered here: the trial is you and the clock. Your best run comes back out as a **ghost** — a see-through copy of the car you set it in, driving the stage again exactly as you drove it, while a chip beside the clock counts the metres of road between you. It cannot be touched: the ghost runs its own game, so there is nothing there to hit. Each stage also keeps a **high score table** — its ten best times, with three letters against each. Land on it and the results card asks for your initials the way a cabinet does: the slots come up holding the name you entered last (**AAA** the first time), typing anything at all replaces it from the first letter, and ENTER posts it. On a phone the chevrons over and under each slot walk the alphabet. **HIGH SCORES** on the time trial page shows every board without driving anything.
- **Roam** — any seed at all. The stage pane shows the map that seed builds as an island of land around the route, turning slowly, with its lakes, hills and forest standing and the **route drawn on it in yellow** (green marks the start, red the finish), so you choose by looking rather than by reading a number. The map is yours to handle: **drag** it to turn it and to tilt it down toward the horizon — which is how you see what "alpine" actually did to it — **scroll or pinch** to zoom in, **hold ⌘/CTRL and drag** (or drag with two fingers, or the middle button) to walk it sideways, and **double-click** to put it back. Zoom keeps going: leaned all the way in the lens is a few metres off the ground, close enough to see the road surface, the trees beside it and the car parked on the start line, and the pan is what gets you to the part of the stage you wanted to look at. It picks its slow turn back up a few seconds after you let go — but only once you have zoomed back out to the whole stage, because a map you have leaned in on is a map you are looking at something on. Previous/next walk the seed; the slider walks the length band (short ≈1 minute, medium ≈3, long ≈5, extra long ≈7 — or **endless**, which streams new road from the seed for as long as you drive), and the STAGE panel beside it — shape, hills, water, forest, tarmac, road width — says what KIND of country the seed builds, with the map redrawing as you press one. **Shape** picks between a SPRINT, which runs from a start line to a finish somewhere else, and a CIRCUIT, which closes back onto its own start line and is raced over three laps — same minutes of driving either way, cut into laps instead of laid out in a line.
- **Options** — the HUD, the renderer, and the controls. See below.

Picking a stage in the campaign or the time trial does not start it: it opens the **pre-race card**, which is where the **car** is chosen. It turns on its stand with the arrows either side of it, and beside it is its **spec sheet** — top speed, 0–100, mass and gears as figures, then bars for acceleration, top speed, traction, braking, tarmac and gravel grip, turn-in and slide. The bars compare the car with the rest of the roster rather than with zero, because what you actually want to know is which of the three is the grippy one and which one wants to be sideways. **TRANSMISSION** is on the same card — AUTO shifts for you, MANUAL holds the gear you chose — and it is the same setting as Options → Controls → GEARBOX, offered where the question is really being asked. Then **START**. Roam keeps its own car picker on the map page, beside the seed it is previewing.

**Time of day** (dawn, day, dusk, night) and **weather** (clear, rain, storm — weather sets the wind, and the wind leans on the car) are yours on Roam and authored into each campaign stage. Off the pre-race card you sit on the line under a rally start gantry — three reds filling a lamp a second, a tick on each, then all green and away.

### Controls

**Desktop (keyboard).** Every one of these is rebindable in Options → Controls — click a binding, press the key you want.

| Key        | Action                                                             |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| ↑ / W      | Throttle                                                           |
| ↓ / S      | Brake — and reverse, stopped                                       |
| ← → / A D  | Steer                                                              |
| Space      | Handbrake — unsticks the rear                                      |
| E / X      | Shift up (manual car)                                              |
| Q / Z      | Shift down (manual car)                                            |
| Esc        | In-race menu — and BACK, in a menu                                 |
| C / V      | Camera: next of six angles                                         |
| M          | Back to the main menu                                              |
| B          | Back to the track (off-road)                                       |
| R          | Restart stage                                                      |
| Enter      | Screenshot — kept in the main menu's GALLERY                       |
| Alt (hold) | Hide the HUD — for a clean look at the game, or a screenshot of it |

**Phone (touch):** one half of the screen is the wheel — touch anywhere and drag sideways to steer. The rim has weight — it turns toward your thumb rather than snapping to it, so a thumb wobble is not a slide and a slight steer is a real option, while a committed shove takes it to full lock in about a quarter second. A blue arc fills the rim from 12 o'clock to the marker, showing how much lock the car has. The other half is the pedal: touching it is GAS, drag down to BRAKE, drag right for the handbrake (DRIFT — unsticks the rear for tight curves). Pulling the thumb back is the car being reined in, pushing it away is the car being sent — the gesture means what it looks like. Keep the brake held once the car has stopped and it backs up, so a nose in a tree is something you drive out of. Which half steers, and which drag does what, are set in Options → Controls; each direction holds one action, so assigning a taken one swaps them. **The manual box takes its gears from the same thumb**: a quick FLICK up is a gear up and a flick down is a gear down — stab off the anchor and straight back off the glass, without ever leaving the throttle for a button. What separates a flick down from the brake bound to the same drag is that a brake is a HOLD: stay down past a quarter second and it is the pedal, and letting go of it costs no gear. A flick only takes a gear the car can use — up once the shift light is on, down once the revs have dropped far enough for the lower ratio to hold the speed — so a stray stab costs nothing. **Two arrows sit just left of your thumb**, up and down, and the one that is lit gold is the gear that is there to take: no need to try it to find out. A key or a pad shoulder is never held to that: those are deliberate, and they shift whenever you ask. A **shutter** button sits at the left of the top bar — the phone's way to the screenshot the keyboard takes with Enter, and it is only offered where there is no keyboard to take one with. Tap the **minimap** for the in-race menu (resume, restart, main menu). Works in portrait and landscape — the HUD re-flows to fit.

**Controller (any platform).** Plug in or pair a pad — an Xbox or DualSense controller on a desktop, a phone with a clip-on, or a handheld like a Retroid Pocket that runs the installed PWA on its own buttons — and press one of its buttons. It appears in Options → Controls, and the on-screen wheel and pedal come off the screen while it is connected (there is a switch for that if you want both).

| Control            | Driving                                                 | In a menu |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------- |
| Left stick         | Steer — analogue, so a small lean is a small correction | Move      |
| D-pad              | Steer, for anyone who would rather not use the stick    | Move      |
| R2 (right trigger) | Throttle — analogue: half a trigger is half the pedal   |           |
| L2 (left trigger)  | Brake — and reverse, stopped                            |           |
| A                  | Handbrake — unsticks the rear                           | Select    |
| B                  | Back to the track (off-road)                            | Back      |
| X                  | Camera: next of six angles                              |           |
| L1 / R1            | Shift down / up (manual car)                            |           |
| Select             | Screenshot                                              |           |
| Start              | In-race menu — and the way back out of it               | Next      |

The **analogue triggers** are the reason a pad is worth driving on: a slide is caught with a fraction of the throttle, which no key can express.

**The menus work on the pad too** — the stick and the d-pad walk the cards, A presses what the cursor is on, B is the way back. A gold ring shows where the cursor is, and holding a direction repeats it. It covers every surface: the main menu, the pre-race card, options, the pause card, the finish card and the results sheet.

**Start is NEXT**, and it is not the same press as A. A presses whatever the cursor is standing on; Start takes the screen's own way on, wherever the cursor happens to be — so pressing it from the front door goes CAMPAIGN, then the stage you are up to, then START, and you are on a start line without having chosen anything. Every card also stands the cursor where it makes sense to start: the campaign tile on the main menu, the stage you are up to on a ladder, the car itself on the pre-race card (left and right over the stand change the car, and the arrows either side of a value work that way everywhere — the seed on Roam, the volume faders in Options), and RESUME on the pause card. At the end of a run Start takes the next stage.

Two actions ship **unbound** on purpose, and for the same reason in each case — there is already a way to do it that cannot be done by accident. **RESTART STAGE**, because it is the one press on the pad that pressing again will not undo. **MAIN MENU**, because Start opens a card that has MAIN MENU on it, and a button that walks out of a run mid-run is the same fumble wearing a different hat. Both are in the list if you want them somewhere.

Everything here is rebindable the same way the keys are: tap a row, press the button. If the car wanders down a straight with nobody touching the stick, raise **stick deadzone**.

### Options

**HUD** switches off anything you do not need on screen — minimap, the rear-view mirror, the on-screen camera button, pacenotes (and separately their WORDS, leaving just the corner signs — each one the shape of the corner it calls, on a plate pointed the way the road goes — to read at a glance), damage glyph, tachometer, stage clock. Speed, gear and the start lights stay: those are the game.

The **camera** key (C or V) walks eight angles, inside out: **COCKPIT** behind the wheel, **HOOD** over your own bonnet, **BUMPER** down at the nose with no bodywork in frame at all, then CLOSE, CHASE, FAR, HELI and TOP. OPTIONS ▸ HUD picks which one every stage opens on — the row opens a card with all of them on it, each with the sentence that says what it is like to drive from, because the choice is between the pictures and not between the words — and, under INSIDE THE CAR, sets up the three you sit in: **SEAT** height, **REACH**, **FIELD OF VIEW**, and **HEAD MOTION**, which is how much the driver's head moves at all. The head has weight: it is thrown forward under the brakes, sideways through a corner, down into a landing and along whatever you just hit, with the road buzzing up through it. HEAD MOTION winds all of that down together, and OFF bolts the camera to the car for anybody the movement does not agree with.

The **rear view** is a strip of glass at the top of the frame showing the road behind, from the car, left-for-right the way a mirror does — and from the CAR whichever angle you are watching from, so switching to the helicopter view does not turn the mirror round with it. From the COCKPIT it is not a strip at all: the same picture is in the mirror hanging in the top of your windscreen, so it swings with the car and moves as your head does. It is a second pass over the stage and the most expensive thing on this list, so it is also the first one to switch off on a device that is struggling — though the game gets there first: the glass is redrawn sixty times a second while the machine is holding sixty frames, and quietly less often (and, in the end, seeing less far) while it is not, so what a slow machine loses is the freshness of the mirror rather than the pace of the road. The **camera button** is only the on-screen one; the camera key (C or V) keeps working with it hidden.

**Screenshots.** Enter — or the shutter on the top bar, on a phone — takes a picture of the frame you are looking at, signed with the game's own mark in the corner. It is the rendered frame rather than the HUD over it: the car, the road, the weather and the mirror, and nothing to say what machine it was taken on. Every picture goes into **GALLERY** on the main menu, where one shot fills the page and the roll runs as a filmstrip under it — the arrow keys or the chevrons flip between them. From there a picture can go to your phone's own share sheet, onto the clipboard, or into your downloads; only the ones your browser can actually do are offered. The roll keeps the last forty and the oldest falls off. Every picture also goes straight onto the **clipboard** as it is taken, so sending one is a paste rather than a trip through the gallery — Options → Controls → COPY TO CLIPBOARD turns that off on its own. Options → Controls → SCREENSHOTS switches the key and the shutter off; both ship on, and the gallery stays reachable either way.

**Video** is the set of levers that buy frames on a weak device: **resolution** (the pixel-ratio ceiling, and the biggest single win), **draw distance** (how far the fog lets you see), **effects** (dust, spray, exhaust, rain and the ambient life), **car interiors** — how much cabin is built behind the glass: OFF puts solid windows back, LOW furnishes the trim, dash, seats and crew, FULL adds the roll cage, the harnesses and a steering wheel that turns with the front tyres — **undergrowth**, the grass, shrubs and stumps scattered between the trees, and **ground detail**, the loose stone: the chippings spilled across the road's edge and the cobbles out in the field. The last three apply to the next stage you start, because all three are baked into geometry. Both scatter settings are picture settings and nothing more: the trees you can HIT are always drawn whatever undergrowth says, and nothing ground detail thins is anything you could hit, so turning either down never makes a stage easier. Ground detail is the biggest lever on this page after resolution — a stage carries thousands of stones — and the road still runs out into the grass on PLAIN, just with fewer of them doing it. How thickly the forest itself stands is a stage dial, set per run in Roam.

**Controls** only ever offers what the device you are on can use: a desktop gets the key bindings, a touch device gets the thumb layout, and a laptop with a touchscreen gets both.

### Developer tools

Tap the car on its stand seven times and a **DEVELOPER** tile appears on the main menu; it stays for good. Besides opening every stage, it holds the two tools that make a problem you can SEE into one somebody else can reproduce — both also on the pause card mid-run, which is where you actually want them:

- **God mode** takes the camera off the car and flies it, on whatever the game is being played on. On a keyboard: `W A S D` to move (forward follows where you look; strafe stays level), `Space`/`E` up, `Ctrl`/`Q` down, `Shift` for a burst of speed, `-` and `=` for the cruise speed, the arrow keys or the mouse (click first) to look. On a **controller**: the stick you steer with moves the rig, the other one aims it, the triggers climb and descend, the handbrake button is the burst of speed, and the shoulders you shift with turn the cruise speed. On a **touchscreen**: the driving zones become fly zones — a push stick under the steering thumb, drag-to-look under the other, and a row of buttons for up, down and the cruise speed. **The run is HELD for as long as you are flying**: the clock stops, the field stops, the weather stops, and the car waits exactly where you left it — flying is for looking at a moment, and a moment that drove on while you looked at it is one you cannot fly back to. Switch god mode off and the run picks up from the same instant.
- **Debug overlay** puts boxes in the corner naming the stage, where the camera is standing, and what the car is doing — and along the bottom, a **REPRO** line: a URL carrying the seed, the generator dials, the conditions and the camera's exact pose. Send a screenshot with that line in it and whoever reads it can open the same frame. Holding Alt hides the game's HUD and leaves these boxes up, which is the shot worth sending. **The boxes go into the picture, not just onto the screen**: with the overlay up, a screenshot has the panels and the REPRO line painted into its pixels, so the file still says where it was taken after it has been pasted into a chat window. Flying with the overlay OFF — which is what you want when the whole frame is the thing being judged — there is a **COPY DEBUG INFO** button in the bottom corner instead: one press and the same facts are on the clipboard as text.

**DEVELOPER → DEBUG LOG** keeps what led there: every engine event with its numbers, and a position line a second. Copy the latest run or the whole buffer.

Once that tile is out, the **frame rate** is also on the HUD for good — under the minimap, beside the stage number, where it can be read while the game is actually being played rather than from behind a wall of debug boxes. The overlay's CAMERA box carries the same number plus what the rear-view mirror is currently costing.

### Installing on your phone

The game is a PWA. iOS Safari: Share → **Add to Home Screen**. Android Chrome: menu → **Install app** (or accept the install prompt). The installed game launches fullscreen, works offline once loaded, and shows an in-app prompt when a new build ships.

### How a run works

Stages are point-to-point: start gate to finish gate. The cluster in the bottom-left is the instrument panel: the car's condition on top (the damage glyph, plus whatever the moment has to say), then rev counter, gear with a shift light, and speed. Hold the engine on its limiter and the rev counter shakes.

The **clock** owns the top-left corner, because in a racing game the clock is the opponent: TOTAL TIME big, and under it — on a circuit — the LAP TIME with the lap counter beside it, then the laps already set this run and the record the stage is holding for you. The **minimap** sits top-right: the whole stage drawn as a route, the car riding it as an arrowhead, and the run's progress read off the frame — the border fills clockwise from the top as you get further in (on a circuit it fills once per lap and reads LAP 2/3; an endless run has no finish to fill toward, so it reads the distance covered instead). In a HEADS UP race the map also carries the race: every rival still out there is a plate on the route with their door number on it, painted the colour of their car, so you can watch the order change instead of waiting to be told about it — and the leader's plate is always the one on top. Tap or click it for the in-race menu — resume, restart, or back to the main menu. The stage number under the map is the seed. On a campaign stage the **position board** sits between the camera button and the map, as big as the clock is — your place out of the fifteen crews entered, as of the last split board you went through; a heads-up race left on one green, so there it counts live. Finishing puts your time on screen; a campaign stage records the clear when the place was good enough for the podium, and the points that clear paid are what open the next one. The card also says what the place was worth, where the location's table stands after it, and — behind FULL RESULTS — the whole field's classification. That sheet is not in until the last car is home, and while it is still coming the card says CARS STILL OUT — and stands over the race that is still going on. You get the flying finish first, the camera planted at the gate watching you go; a few seconds down the run-out it leaves your car and watches the crew behind you instead, from high up, the way the main menu watches a stage. **SPECTATE** takes you down to them: the camera flies to the car in a second, in an arc over whatever country is in the way, and lands in the chase view you raced under — and the card comes down for the HUD you just raced under, reading THEIR car. Their clock top left, and under it how many seconds they are up or down on you at the last split you have both been through; their place and their route top right; their revs, gear, speed and dents bottom left. Above the car, where the co-driver's calls would be, a banner names the crew, and the arrows either side of it walk the field a car at a time. It cuts to the next car by itself when the one you are watching crosses the line. BACK TO RESULTS puts the card up whenever you have seen enough and the race carries on behind it. The classification is the same one either way — and pressing on to the next stage before the last car is home still records it in full.

**Off the road is a real place.** The landscape around every stage is driveable — forests, hills, mountains, streams, lakes and open sea — and the car rides it fast (up to ~150 km/h in the wild; ~230 km/h flat out on the road). Cliff edges and banks throw the car if you hit them with pace; boulders and fallen trunks are genuinely solid, and deep water swallows the car whole. A crash puts you back on the track where you left it. There is no off-road timer: explore as long as you like, and press **B** when you want back.

**Drifting** is not a button — it is just what the car does when you turn harder than the tires can hold, which past about 70 km/h is any committed turn. Steer into the bend and the car goes sideways while the road keeps flowing: steering into it deepens it, lifting the throttle tightens it, counter-steering gathers it up. It costs you almost no speed, so there is nothing to count and nothing to cash in — just keep it flowing. The handbrake is there to unstick the rear in something really tight. **Jumps** throw you off ramps; midair the car barely answers, so line up before the lip and land straight or lose speed. **Fords** splash and drag; **crests** can go light at full speed.

## Developing

```sh
git clone https://github.com/niclaslindstedt/game2
cd game2
npm install        # needs a GitHub Packages token — see docs/configuration.md
npm run dev
```

Then read [architecture.md](architecture.md) for the layout and [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) for the workflow. The three tools you'll live in while tuning: `make sim` (does the change help or hurt, measured), `make track` (what the generator builds), `make screenshots` (what it looks like).
