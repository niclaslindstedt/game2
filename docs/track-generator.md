# The track generator

Stages are built by a rules engine (`engine/mapgen/`), not authored by hand. The design splits into three files with three jobs:

- **`rules.ts` — the rule book.** Every constraint and every vocabulary number lives here as data: what a soft, medium, or hard turn may be, how long straights run, where jumps, crossings, and crests may sit, the length bands and their world bounds, the self-distance floor, the six dials (below). Tuning the generator means editing this file.
- **`biomes.ts` — the countries (R40).** What a stage is built IN, as rows: the region and grove quilt each country is planted from, whether it holds water, whether its woods shed timber, what its bladed roads are made of, how low its relief is worn and whether the wind has piled sand across it, which weathers its sky has and where on earth it lies. The taiga's row is the country every other rule was written against; the desert's is the one with none of the water. Nothing else in `mapgen/` names a country — the geology, the prop field and the search read the rows.
- **`search.ts` — the machinery every search shares.** The walking cursor, the spatially hashed point field that answers R9 and R10, the draws that turn the vocabulary into a candidate segment, and the feature assignment that decides what a straight carries. What differs between the three searches is only where the line is being steered.
- **`generate.ts` — the sprint search.** Draws candidate segments from the seeded RNG, validates each against the rules, retries a bounded number of times, backtracks when boxed in, and rejects a whole attempt (retrying with a derived sub-seed) rather than ever shipping a violation. `generateStage(seed, length, knobs, shape)` is a pure function of its inputs.
- **`endless.ts` — the stream.** The same vocabulary walked forever instead of closed. A stream has nowhere to be and can never start over, so everything it does about a country that boxes it in happens in place: a course it steers back onto, a commit lag that is its backtrack runway, and a water setback it walks down a rung at a time. `createStageStream(seed)` hands out frozen road as it goes.
- **`circuit.ts` — the closed lap (R22).** The same vocabulary steered around a RING — a bearing that turns once through a full circle over the target lap — and then CLOSED onto the grid's own pose by a solved turn-straight-turn. See [The circuit](#the-circuit).
- **`road.ts` — the cross-section.** What a road looks like ACROSS its width: the camber, the corner's bank, the two worn wheel tracks, the asphalt mat standing proud of the ground, the shoulder falling away into the field. One module read by three consumers — the renderer builds the ribbon from it, the terrain field hangs its verge off it, and the physics rides it — so the shape the car climbs out of is exactly the shape the player sees.
- **`highway.ts` — the public roads (R17).** The tarmac, laid across the seed BEFORE the route exists: whole roads that come in over one edge of the map and leave over another, following the same country and keeping the same clearances as everything else. They are the landscape's, not the rally's, and everything after this has to work around them.
- **`borrow.ts` — turning onto one.** The turn-straight-turn that gets the route from where it is onto a public road at a real corner, the run it spends there, and the solve that gets it off again. What the `asphalt` dial actually spends.
- **`crossing.ts` — going straight over one (R36).** The other answer to a road in the way, and the cheap one: the solve that lines the route up square on a public road and the straight that carries it across. Costs no detour and none of the `asphalt` dial, so a stage that never wanted tarmac is not partitioned by the roads its country happens to carry.
- **`land.ts` — the bare country.** The landscape before anybody laid a road across it: rises, hills, mountain chains, and the basins that fill to lakes. The terrain field shapes itself around the road from it, and the branch builder steers by it — a road has to know where the water is before it drives into it.
- **`spurs.ts` / `guards.ts` / `river.ts` — the country around the route.** The abandoned branches at every junction, the mounds and groves that shut the inside of a sharp corner, and the watercourses the road crosses.
- **`railway.ts` — the railway, and the train on it (R41).** The line is laid by `highway.ts` as a second kind of public line (`kind: "rail"`), before the route, at a railway's radii, held off the roads; the route crosses it by `crossing.ts`'s own solve, and this module owns what the crossing then needs: the record on the track, the line the train runs (the two arms cut from the railway and joined through the crossing), the timetable drawn per crossing, and the train as the physics meets it — a run of moving solids standing on the rails wherever the train is at this second, a pure function of the stage clock.
- **`homesteads.ts` — the country somebody lives in (R37).** The houses off the stage: where a yard fits at the end of a short gravel drive that meets the rally road square, which house stands on it (as a plan the renderer builds from), the cars outside it, the lane trees down the drive, and the barrier across the drive's mouth. Their drives ride the branch index for their shelf and their keep-off; their yards are pads the terrain flattens; their walls, cars and trunks are solids.
- **`buildings.ts` — what stands on a yard or along a street, as plans.** The building vocabulary both placers draw from: kind (house, villa, block of flats, grocery, post office, workshop), footprint, storeys, roof, paint, porch, wing — and the walk that turns any footprint into the run of wall bays the car stops against.
- **`towns.ts` — where the tarmac leads (R39).** The small towns: which piece of sealed road holds one (the run the rally borrows, or the arm the tape shuts at a junction), the lots down both sides of it with a building on each and the cars outside, and the shops in the middle. Every lot is a pad the terrain grades level with the street's verge; the walls and the cars are solids.
- **`carparks.ts` — where the crowd parked (R42).** Placed on the terrain field beside the stands rather than in the compiler, because it is planned FROM the stands: for each one, a pad found by walking out from it over the country map (`carpark-map.ts` — a coarse lattice asked whether a road may be driven through a cell and whether a person may walk across it), a lane from the pad square off a public road within reach or, failing that, searched out over the same map to a road already there or to the edge of the map, the bays and the cars on the pad, and the trails threaded from the pad to every stand the walk reaches, with the arrow boards along them. The pad is a pad the terrain grades to a plane, the lane rides the branch index for its shelf and its keep-off, the trails keep the forest off, and the cars are solids.
- **`traffic.ts` — the country does not stop for the rally (R44).** The routes over the public roads the stage does not close: the arms past their barriers and the car park lanes, cut at every place they reach and joined into one polyline per journey on the driver's own side, with the speed limit signs stood where the limit changes. The fleet that drives them is the game's (`engine/game/traffic.ts`): twenty vehicle models as data, each on a route at the posted limit, keeping its distance, and carrying a `CarState` so the player's car meets it through the same two-body capsule contact a rival is met through.
- **`energy.ts` — the country makes power (R43).** The wind farms and the solar farms, in a country modern enough to have them (`BiomeRules.energy`): a string of three to seven turbines on the highest dry ground a fan of probes off the stage can find — every foot over the road it is seen from, off every road by more than a rotor, on a crane pad the terrain flattens — and a fenced rectangle of panel tables on level ground beside the road, every one facing the sun's azimuth, from a paddock's worth to a field of them. The towers, the tables, the fence posts and the inverter cabin are solids; a solar farm is a clearing the forest keeps off; the watercourses steer round both.
- **`compile.ts` — the geometry.** Turns the segment plan into evenly spaced samples (position, heading, elevation, surface, curvature, jump lips) plus the pacenote list the HUD calls from — the single geometric truth read by the physics, the renderer, and the bots alike. `compileStage(seed, length)` is the entry point; an endless track carries a `track.extend(upToS)` that materializes more road as the run progresses.

## The R-rules

The generator respects rally reality. Verbatim from the rule book, each enforced in the search (or realized in the compiler) and asserted across seeds in `tests/mapgen_test.ts`:

- **R1** A stage opens with a straight — a start grid plus room to build speed. The length is set by what the straight has to hold: a heads-up race stands its whole field on the apron behind the gate (`startZone.apron`, sized from `TUNING.massStart.rowGap` for a sixteen-car grid), so the road off the line carries the grid's own depth plus `launch.run` before the first corner, and that first corner is drawn from the `launch.firstTurn` severity or easier. A grid sixteen rows deep arriving at a hairpin still stacked is a pile-up, not a start.
- **R2** A finite stage closes with a straight — a visible finish, no blind final turn. (An endless stage never closes.)
- **R3** Turns come in three severities with distinct radius/angle vocabularies: soft (fast, open), medium (a real corner), and hard (slow, tight — up to hairpin).
- **R4** A hard turn only follows a straight: there is always a braking zone. Straight by R38's measure — a borrowed road's bend gentler than `straightRun.bend` is walked as a turn of the road's own radius and is a braking zone all the same.
- **R5** At most two consecutive same-direction turns — no endless spirals — and a same-direction run never curls past the angle cap (a near-loop the self-distance probe would only reject after building the doomed geometry).
- **R6** Jumps sit on straights, with a clear run-up before the lip and a landing zone after it; jumps keep a minimum spacing between lips. And a jump lands on road that is still there: the search reads the line it walks past the lip, and a road falling away under the flight by more than `jump.landingFall` is no place for one — every metre the ground drops is a metre more of flight, and a gentle ramp on a downhill came out as a ninety-metre jump.
- **R7** Water (a ford) sits on straights only, never in the landing zone of a jump, and never on the opening or closing straight.
- **R8** Crests (blind brows) sit on straights and never combine with a jump or a ford on the same segment.
- **R9** A finite stage's centerline stays inside its length's world bounds; when the stage nears the boundary the generator must turn back toward the middle. (An endless stage roams an unbounded world.)
- **R10** The centerline never crosses itself — non-adjacent parts of the stage keep a minimum distance between them (measured per candidate point beyond an 80 m route-neighbour window, so hairpins stay legal and folds stay impossible). The distance itself is R23's. On an endless stage the guarantee covers the trailing `endless.tailWindow` meters — road further back is long gone behind the car and out of sight.
- **R11** Total stage length lands inside the selected stage length's band.
- **R12** A ford sits in a dip: the road eases down to FLAT water and climbs back out. Water never stands on a rise — it collects at a local low point, fed by the stream that crosses the road there. So a ford LIES IN ITS VALLEY: its water is laid at the lowest of the bare land at the road and across it either side (`water.bankReach`), a pool the road wades and the river holds the level of through the crossing on both sides, and the road dips down to it from wherever its line was running over an apron as long as that drop needs to stay a splash a car drops into (`water.apronGrade`; each apron sized on its own, because a ford on a grade has its water at the lower mouth and the whole fall to lose at the other). The search only lets a straight carry a ford whose aprons fit inside it. Where the road stands too far over the water to dip to it (`water.culvert.fordDrop`) it stays on its fill and the stream goes UNDER it, through a CULVERT: no dip, no water on the road, the same water at the same level on both sides, and a pipe's mouth in each side of the embankment (`track.culverts`; the renderer stands the pipe). Laid at the road's own height instead, as it was, a ford on an embankment anchored its river metres over the country and the sheet on the road stood a metre over the river running off it — a step in a water surface, which is the one thing water never does. The channel remains visible beyond both road edges, so a full-width wet road reads as a road through a stream rather than a puddle painted onto the ribbon.
- **R13** A water crossing too wide to wade carries a BRIDGE instead of a ford: the road stays level across it, the water runs in a ravine below, and the deck is timber up to `bridge.timberMax` — past that only concrete spans it. Going over the side is a drowning, which is what a parapet is for — so a concrete deck's parapet is an unbroken run of SOLIDS down both edges, cast into the deck, immovable, and drawn exactly where the engine collides with it (`bridgeParapets`, the one statement of where the bays stand). It is the one wall on a stage that is there on purpose: R31 cuts every other one away. A timber deck's rail is posts and a rail, and a car goes through it. Every leg that carries a deck — the piers and both abutments — is founded on the GROUND under its own foot rather than on a nominal water level, or the bridge reads from the bank as one held up by nothing.
- **R14** The inside of a sharp corner is GUARDED: a turn (or a combination) that bends past `guard.angle` gets the ground between its entry and its exit filled with a steep mound or a dense grove. Neither is a wall — both can be taken — but both cost more than the corner does, which is the point: a corner whose inside is open grass is not a corner, it is a suggestion.
- **R15** Asphalt is not a SURFACE the stage puts on, it is a ROAD the stage borrows. The public roads are laid across the country before the route exists (R17), so a sealed length is always a stretch the route turned onto and back off again — hundreds of metres long, never a chequerboard — and the `asphalt` dial is what the route may SPEND on borrowing rather than a share painted on afterwards. The one length of tarmac that is not a borrow is R20's run-out.
- **R16** A road has a CROSS-SECTION, and it is CURVED across its width rather than flat: FIVE LINES run down it — a loose pale edge on each side that no wheel ever touches, two worn tracks a real car's track-width apart where every car before you put its wheels, and the crown between them, driven over but never grooved and so the highest line on the road. The tracks are troughs deep enough to feel and to see, which is what tells you from inside the car that the road has been used; the loose edges fade into the shoulder rather than stopping at it, so the surfacing meets the country instead of ending at a ruled line. Asphalt is laid ON the ground so its mat stands proud with chippings down the edge. Past the shoulder the ground simply leans away into the field. No ditch — a trench ruled down both sides reads as a scar cut by a machine, and it is a trap that swallows a car the moment it puts a wheel wide. And no EDGE either: past the bare shoulder the road RUNS OUT. Its surface hands over to the ground lattice beside it over the last few metres of the corridor (`handoverAt`) so the two meet at a shared height instead of the ribbon stopping in the air over whatever the lattice happened to be doing — that step was a vertical face down the length of every stage, a median of 0.4 m and, where a road crossed a lattice cell diagonally, over ten. Its colour hands over the same way, dissolving into the ground's own paint along a noise field rather than fading to a fixed green across a band of fixed width, because a boundary ruled parallel to the centerline is legible from the far side of a valley. Its LIFT hands over too — the hair that keeps the mat off the ground lattice is spent by the lip, or the two meshes meet two centimetres apart and the gap between them is a hairline down the whole stage. Its GRAIN hands over as well, and that one is a map rather than a palette: the road's speckle averages a little over half brightness and the ground's nearly all of it, so two vertices painted the same colour render forty per cent apart, and the vertex cancels its own map by however much of the ground's colour it has taken. And past all of that the COUNTRY meets it halfway: the ground beside a gravel road wears the dust off it, fading out over nine metres of open grass, because a hand-over that finished at the lip left the country going back to full meadow green in one vertex. Then the stones do the rest. They lie on the mat itself, everywhere except the two lines the wheels have swept clean, they run off it and thin away over ten metres of grass, and tufts of grass grow back the other way over the loose margin no wheel touches — all three on the one noise field, so they interlock instead of each drawing an edge of its own. The ratio goes from all gravel to all grass, and nowhere along it is there a line.
- **R17** TARMAC IS LAID BEFORE THE STAGE IS. The public roads belong to the country rather than to the rally: `highway.ts` draws them across the seed before the route exists, whole, each one running in over one edge of the map and out over another, following the same ground and obeying the same clearances as everything else — R23's keep from every road already laid, and from ITSELF: a road that went round a lake and came back up its own line is refused and walked again. They are the civilization the country has — the houses and the towns will stand along them — and the rally road is what comes afterwards. A route may TURN ONTO one and run on it for a while (`borrow.ts` solves the turn-straight-turn that gets it there and the one that gets it off again, and between the two the route walks the road's own bends however gentle — a bend R38 counts as straight is still driven, it is only not called), and it may MEET one at a junction. What it may never do is WANDER ACROSS one (R36 is the one way over), or turn half of one to gravel because a stage went that way: both are checked as the route is drawn, and `roads.cross` measures the result on the built stage. A surface change is therefore always a JUNCTION, and a junction is a PLACE. It sits ON the route's centerline, at a corner tight enough that the two carriageways actually part (`paving.junctionParts`, measured in road widths) rather than peel apart over a slip road's worth of tangent. The sealed road is the MAIN road: it runs straight through, made of the route's own collinear arm on one side and the abandoned branch — the same road, the same width — on the other. Its markings run straight past the crossing: a sealed road whose lines stop for fifty metres at every junction reads as two roads dissolving into each other, which is the thing this rule exists to prevent. The gravel road is the MINOR one, and it stops AT the main road's edge, cut at that angle, with the smear of gravel every car turning out of it drags onto the seal — it never runs on underneath the tarmac and out the far side. Approaching that edge its mat opens into a MOUTH (`junction.mouth`): a quarter ellipse that widens over a road's length of lane and is at its widest exactly where the two meet, which is where a car leaving the dirt road needs the room to turn either way. Inside the platform both carriageways are warped onto one graded plane: no camber, no bank, no wheel tracks, no borders. The abandoned branch is real road — TARMAC end to end, because a sealed road that turns to gravel in an empty field is a road that goes nowhere — and it runs off the map. Where the junction stands on a public road it IS that road, cut from it at the meeting point and followed to its end (`cutSpur`), which is why it gets there; where the seal is the route's own, the branch is built out from the corner instead and the corner has to pass a test before it may carry a junction at all. Either way the arm leaves ON the main road's plane — the junction's height and grade, held for `PLATFORM_HOLD` of its arc, as far as the platform's warp reaches — before it follows the country at a minor road's grade and crest rule (`followStep`), aiming at the stage's shelf band rather than being clamped to it; asked inside the platform, where the cone has no swing, the band is degenerate and a clamp to it drops the arm two metres at its first step: the branch is trial-built against the country the plan describes, and one whose abandoned arm cannot get clear is not used. The trial is never allowed to be more optimistic than the build — a corner accepted on an arm the compiler then cuts short leaves a stub of tarmac standing in a field. A player who ignores the tape can follow it. It is SHUT with a barrier — cones, stacks of scrap tyres, round bales or oil drums, rolled per branch, with the chevron board over all four saying which way the stage does go. Where that barrier stands is the generator's (`placeBlock`), not the renderer's, and the reason is that it is a thing standing in front of a driver at speed: the whole LINE of it has to clear the route, at both ends, or the sign telling somebody which way to go is the thing they hit going that way. Placed at the branch's first sample off the platform it was square across the road the stage takes on a third of a sweep of seeds. A branch too short, or folded too closely against the route, to take a barrier that clears it is left open — nothing in the way beats something in the way. ONE surface change on a stage is not a junction, and R20 owns it: where a seal reaches a corner no public road would have been laid with, the surfacing runs out there instead.
- **R18** Water obeys nature: ONE watercourse per valley, born on the high ground above the highest crossing, visiting every place the road crosses it in descending order, gathering width as it goes, pooling flat where the ground dips, and ending in the lowest water it can find. Two crossings the land refuses to join — a ridge between them — are on different water. And it meets the road at its crossings and nowhere else — a ford, a deck or a culvert (R12), each an anchor the course is routed through at the valley's own level: between two of them the course keeps its bank clear of the corridor, because water routed under a road anywhere else carves the ground out from under the ribbon and draws a sheet of water through it. It leaves a crossing ACROSS the road — a road on an embankment is a ridge, and the downhill from a point on its centerline runs along it — and it is traced against the bare country, not the ground the road shaped: a course following an embankment's flank down is water the analyzer, which measures it against the bare banks the way the world sees it, reports ten metres in the air. Beside a road the channel goes no deeper than the road's own fill slope (`terrain.ts`'s carve floor), so a stream at the toe of a fill has the fill for its near bank rather than a bed cut into it. A reach that cannot get around a road ends there, and the crossings past it are on their own water. And it never runs back over itself: where the downhill it feels and the road pushing it away disagree across a cell boundary, a walk can settle into swapping between the pair with its surface pinned at the floor of the hollow they share — a limit cycle nothing else could end, which draws four hundred points and a full-width sheet of standing water on one spot instead of a river. A walk that returns to ground it has covered stops there, and what water with nowhere left to run does is pool (`water.retrace` measures it).
- **R19** Turns are BANKED. A road built through a corner is superelevated: the cross-fall rolls out of the crown and into the turn over a runoff, tops out at a rate read off the corner's radius, and rolls back out again. Never a wall of a bank — the ceiling is a road a car could be parked on. Gravel and tarmac get the tilt for DIFFERENT REASONS, and both the ceiling and the radius that earns half of it differ accordingly. A sealed road's cross-fall is designed: a highway engineer superelevates the corners that need it and leaves the rest on their camber, so it is reserved for tight geometry. A gravel road's is worn — every car that has ever turned here has pushed loose stone from the inside of the bend to the outside, and that happens on any corner at all — so the gravel banks harder and starts banking at a much longer radius. That second half is what stops a stage of fourth-gear sweepers reading as flat ground with a line painted on it, and `drive`'s `tilt` check is what measures it.
- **R20** A JUMP never sits on sealed road, and neither does a HAIRPIN. A tarmac section is a public road the rally borrows: nobody builds a launch ramp into one, and a highway authority laying a road out for traffic that is not racing sweeps rather than doubling back. So the sealed road takes no turn from R3's `hard` bucket — the tight stuff is the rally's, and the rally's road is the gravel. It is enforced at the JOIN: the route will not turn onto the tarmac if the run it would spend there has a corner tighter than `paving.minRadius` in it, which costs nothing because the surface change already waits for a corner to happen at. Where a seal reaches one anyway — and it can, because where a seal ENDS depends on whether the arm it would abandon can leave the map, a question no cheap look-ahead can answer — the SURFACING RUNS OUT at that corner's start. That is the one surface change on a stage that is not a junction, and it is the smaller of two lies: a length of tarmac that simply stops is what every rural road in the world does when the money ran out, and a main road doubling back on itself is not.
- **R21** The road's WIDTH is a dial, from a narrow lane the trees crowd to a broad boulevard with room to place the car.
- **R22** A stage is SHAPED as a sprint or as a CIRCUIT. A circuit's last sample lands back on its first, on the same heading, so the start line is also the finish line and the stage can be raced over laps. Everything else — the vocabulary, R3 through R8, the world bound, the features — is the sprint's; R10's self-distance is measured cyclically, because on a ring the road running back into the start line is that line's neighbour and not a crossing of it.
- **R23** No two pieces of road share ground. The terrain lays its shelf under ONE road (`terrain.ts` picks the nearest), so a second corridor over the same country is left hanging in the air with nothing under it and nothing to drive on — a wall of road you can see through and drive through. So the clearance is measured centerline to centerline and sized from the road's own width: both corridors' full reach plus `roadClear.margin` of bare country between them. It binds IN HEIGHT as well (`armSeparation` in `search.ts`): two arms of the route that pass each other need the room for the ground between them to get from one to the other at the grade R31 allows beside a road — the upper arm's corridor, the lower arm's bench, and `verge.climb` between the two — or no ground satisfies both and the difference stands up as a face along the upper road's edge. Read against R34's steepest cut face instead, as it was, the rule let a stage fold back forty-five metres from itself with thirty-three metres between the arms, which was the commonest error the analyzer found on the taiga. The search walks the road's own surface for it — the country's height followed by the builder's eye plus the stage's roll (`rolling.ts`, read by the search and the compiler alike). It binds the route against itself (R10), it binds the abandoned branches (R17), which turn away from the stage the way they turn away from a lake and stop where they cannot, and it binds the route against the public roads laid before it. Its one exemption is a JUNCTION, and — because a junction is a PLACE — the exemption is a place too: within `junction.parting` of the point two roads met at they ARE one road, and anywhere else, including further along those same two roads, they are not. Stated as a WINDOW OF STAGE ARC instead, as it was, it forgave every piece of route within a few hundred metres of arc no matter where on the ground that route had wandered to, and the analysis forgave exactly the same stretch — so four branches lying within a metre of the route, one of them 5.9 m below it, went unreported across seeds 1-12.
- **R24** The START is a PLACE, not a line. The grid, the APRON of dirt behind it — real road, with a terrain shelf under it and physics on it — and R23's clearance around both belong to the start: on a sprint the route may not come back into it, no branch may cross it, and the finish's run-out may not land in it. A road floating over the start is the first thing a run ever sees. A circuit is the exception the shape makes: it closes onto its own start line along the apron rather than across it (R22). Past the apron, at either end of a sprint, the stage is simply over and the terrain owns the ground.
- **R25** A SPRINT's finish line is not the end of its road. It carries on past the gate for a RUN-OUT (`runOut`, 220 m) — road the car coasts down after the clock has stopped, so the finish is a line drawn across a road rather than the cliff edge of a world that ran out of budget. The run-out is NOT part of the raced stage: R11's length band measures the road up to the line, and `track.finishS` is where that line is. Crossing it puts the run into its `rollout` phase, and the car is driven home from there (`TUNING.rollOut`) with the camera planted at the gate. A circuit needs none — its finish is its own start line, with a whole lap of road already the other side of it — so it finishes at the line the way it always has.
- **R26** KERBING goes where a driver needs it and nowhere else. See [the placement guide](#kerb-placement-r26) below.
- **R27** A stage is WATCHED. Spectators gather where a rally crowd actually gathers — at the finish, and at the corners worth standing at — on ground clear of the road, on the OUTSIDE of the bend where nothing leaving the road is coming at them. Driving past a stand at pace is heard (`cheer`).
- **R28** A stage is SPLIT INTO CHECKPOINTS, roughly a quarter-minute of driving apart, and every one of them stands just past the EXIT of a corner — the tighter the better. A checkpoint is both a split (where the run is measured against whoever it is racing) and the place a lost, drowned or crashed car is put back on the road, so it belongs where the road has just asked the driver a question rather than in the middle of a straight where it would cost nothing. Preferred, not required: past `checkpoint.forced` gaps' worth of road with no corner worth taking, a board goes down wherever the road has got to. A kilometre of borrowed public road (R17) sweeps and asks the driver nothing, and a stage with no split on it for a kilometre has a clock with no shape. EVERY BOARD IS A GATE, AND THE STAGE IS THE WHOLE OF THEM, IN ORDER. A board counts when the car drives THROUGH it — across its line, between its ends (`checkpoint.gate`), going the way the stage goes — and the next one due is the only one a car can take, so the boards are collected in the order they stand in. The finish line does not end a run, and a circuit's start line does not book a lap, until every one of them is behind the car: a stage cut short across country is not a stage that was driven. See [checkpoint placement](#checkpoint-placement-r28) below.
- **R32** The GROUND comes in LAYERS, laid in the order the country was made (`engine/mapgen/geology.ts`). First BEDROCK — broad swell, hills, mountain chains along their ridges, and the fault steps between them — carrying a per-seed SMOOTHNESS that says which country the stage is in: Sweden and Norway are the same rock, and what separates them is that the ice sat on one and ran off the other, so a smooth country is planed into whaleback summits and filled valleys with none of the fine grain left, and a rough one stands its mountains higher and keeps its texture. Whatever the smoothness, THE ROCK IS CURVES: the ground is drawn on a 14 m lattice, and a crease in the field is a fold on it that reads from a kilometre up, so a crest is a whaleback (a parabola over the ridge noise, not a smoothstep over its fold, which looks rounded and turns over inside one cell wherever the noise runs steep) and a fault step is a worn slope unless somebody asked otherwise. The sharp things a country can have — the alpine knife-edge along a crest, the fault step standing as a cliff — are opened by the `steepness` dial past its midpoint (`geology.steep.crease`) and by nothing else, growing with the dial and with how far down the sharp band the seed's smoothness fell; the one sharp thing it has regardless is the bank of a kettle hole, whose rim is narrower than the lattice can curve (`pits.sharpRim`). The geology says where each of them is (`sharpAt`), so the analysis can hold everything else to a curve. Then GROUNDWATER: a table that follows the land without its detail, shallow under flats and hollows and deep under anything steep, because steep ground drains — where it surfaces the ground is a mire. Then SOIL on top: till and washed sediment, collecting in the hollows and stripped off the mountain flanks and the escarpment faces. And then the PITS — the hollows that hold standing water, in three sizes, because the water bodies they make are three different things: a pit's floor is cut toward the water table, so how far below it lands is what decides what fills it. A broad, barely-cut one is a wide sheet of shallow water, which is a SWAMP — reeds and sedge stand in it, you can see the bottom, and a car can wade it. A few hundred metres across and properly deep is a lake; small and deep is a kettle hole. Pits form only where the table is already near the surface — flat lowland — because a hollow gouged into a mountainside drains and one cut on a summit is a crater. What the layers decide is what is ON them: a tree needs a rooting depth and bare rock grows moss, grass and flowers and nothing with a trunk; loose stone lies on the surface where the cover is thin and is buried where it is deep. Everything is analytic and computed in one pass over shared noise, because `surfaceAt` is read for every ground lattice corner, every prop and every water query in the running game.
- **R33** A GRAVEL road is not smooth, and a SEALED one is. The difference is how they are built. Tarmac is laid by a paving machine, and a plane is what it should be — a sealed section is a public road the rally borrowed, and it reads as one precisely because it is the smooth part. Gravel is bladed, driven on, frozen and bladed again, and that leaves a good surface with things wrong with it HERE AND THERE: a frost heave, a hollow worn at a corner exit, a stone the blade rode over. So the bumps are SPARSE impulses, not a continuous grain — a continuous grain is a washboard however small you make it, wrong in the same way an evenly-sprinkled forest is wrong, because real defects come in ones with clean road between them. Each is a raised cosine, meeting the road at zero height and zero slope so it joins the surface instead of stepping onto it, and each is marginal: a few centimetres is a road with a surface, ten is a pothole. The band of scales the bumps cover is the point of them, and it is why they run from a couple of metres up to a frost heave the length of a house: the road's own rolling profile is hundreds of metres long and the country it follows is longer still, so without them there is NOTHING at the ten-to-thirty-metre scale — which is exactly the scale a car reads as the road being uneven. The gravel's WIDTH is not one number either. A dirt road is cut TIGHT — a fifth under the width the stage is nominally built at, because every metre of it had to be cut and has to be bladed again every spring — and it is then given three things back: two slow waves that open it out and pinch it in along the stage, and the CORNERS, which are wider because everything long enough to need a sweep has widened them, and because a drift needs somewhere to go. The width rolls in and out over a runoff exactly as the bank does, since a mat that gained a metre inside one 2 m sample would be a notch cut in the side of the road. Sealed road, bridge decks and the graded aprons of fords take none of it: no bumps, and the nominal width exactly.
- **R31** The road and the ground beside it are RIDEABLE. Within a BENCH of a road — the route's or an abandoned branch's — the landscape never stands above that road's own corridor, and past the bench it may only rise at a grade the car can climb (`verge.climb`). Whatever the country was doing there is CUT where it would otherwise be a wall a car sliding off the road stops dead against, or a hillside the ground lattice drags up through the tarmac. The bench is a LATTICE CELL DIAGONAL wide (`verge.bench`, against `GROUND_CELL`) because that is the reach of the triangles the ground is drawn and driven on: pin every corner that could sit over a road, and no triangle can cut up through one. It is a CEILING and nothing else — a valley, a ford's dip and the ravine under a bridge are all still exactly as deep as the landscape made them. What it cuts is the LANDSCAPE: a cut is taken against the road the ground is beside, and it never reaches in under a DIFFERENT road's own shelf, because one road's rideability is not a licence to hollow out the ground another is standing on. Without that clause a branch running sixty metres away and twenty metres below took fourteen metres of hillside out from under the route and left its ribbon hanging in the air. R23 gains a height clause from it: a branch standing tens of metres above the stage needs the room for the stage's cone to climb to it, or the hillside that carried it there is gone and the branch is in the air. And the cone LIFTS OFF before its reach ends (`verge.fade`): a cone is a min, and a min that simply stops being asked past its reach ends in a wall — the country standing however high it stands one query cell further out, ruled dead straight along the lattice, which beside a mountain was fifty metres of vertical rock two hundred metres from any road. So over the last stretch of its reach the cone's grade climbs from `verge.climb` to the steepest face rock is ever held at, and a cut runs out into the country as a face that steepens into rock rather than one that stops; a branch's cone is held to the reach its index is guaranteed to find it within, for the same reason.
- **R34** A road is laid ALONG the country, and where it cannot go round something it is CUT THROUGH it. Two halves, and the second only means anything because of the first. The road's height FOLLOWS the bare landscape (`elevation.follow`): a lag that takes the broad shape of the country and ignores every hummock, and two clamps — the gradient anything will drive up, and the vertical curvature that rounds a hilltop into a crest instead of a ramp. So a stage runs down the valleys, the way roads do, and where the country is steeper than the clamps the difference is CUT AND FILL: the embankment across a hollow, the cutting through a shoulder. The road's own rolling noise rides on that rather than being the whole of its height — and the LAG is what decides how uneven a stage is, which is worth saying out loud because the noise looks like the knob for it and is not: over seeds 1-24 the roll contributes about 5 m of travel per km against the country's 29. A long lag is a highway engineer running a graded line across a landscape and reading nothing under a quarter of a kilometre, which over a whole stage reads as a ribbon laid on a picture of a country; a short one is a man following the ground with a bulldozer. `drive`'s `rolling` check measures the result in metres of climb and descent per kilometre. It refuses to follow the country into water — a lake is crossed on an embankment, never along the bed of one. Then the cuttings: R31 holds the country beside a road to a grade a car can climb, and taken alone that is a country with no rock in it, every shoulder battered back to the same ramp. So the grade R31 holds it to is the ANGLE OF REPOSE OF WHAT IS THERE — deep till slumps back to `verge.climb`, rock stands at its own face — and how far it is worth blasting depends on what the road is worth: a sealed road was engineered and takes the shoulder out of the way, a gravel road was scraped in by a grader and a grader goes round. `knobs.steepness` says how hard the country is on both counts. The face begins a whole lattice cell OUTSIDE R31's bench, so the flat runoff a car that goes wide needs is untouched and the rock stands beyond it; and the two sides of a road are one number read with opposite signs, because a road across a slope is benched into it — cut on the uphill side, filled on the downhill — which is what makes a cutting a place you pass through instead of a trench you drive down.
- **R35** The water is POURED ONTO THE COUNTRY BEFORE ANY ROAD IS DRAWN, and where it settles is decided by physics rather than by a single sea level. Rain falls on the bare ground of R32, runs downhill, and collects: a priority flood finds the SILL every hollow would spill over, the groundwater table says how much of that hollow is actually wet, and a body's surface is the lower of the two — filled to its rim where the groundwater would push it higher, standing at the groundwater where the rim is higher still. So every body has its OWN LEVEL (a tarn on a shoulder sits nowhere near the sea), each surface is FLAT across that body, and the SHORELINE is the ground's own contour: the level is worked out on a coarse grid and the waterline is settled against the analytic ground, so a lake's edge follows every headland the rock makes at whatever resolution anybody draws it. Because the water is there first, everything downstream can ask about it instead of assuming: the stage's ORIGIN is sited on ground clear of it (no start line in a lake), the route SEARCH refuses any line that comes within `water.routeClear` metres of a body — a setback across the ground, not a height, because a height margin pins a road to the top of the bank, which is the one place R31's verge cannot be cut — the road's own freeboard clears the water that is actually there rather than one table for the whole world, and a watercourse ENDS in whatever body it reaches and can be steered toward it. Where a country is too wet to yield a stage at the full setback the search walks it down `water.routeClearLadder` rather than failing; a stage crossing water is the last resort, not the default it used to be.
- **R36** A stage may CROSS a public road, and the only way it may do it is SQUARE. Not a junction: nobody turns. The gravel arrives at right angles (`crossing.ts` solves the turn-straight-turn that lines the route up on a pose `crossing.clear` metres short of the road pointing across it, then a straight carries it over), goes straight over the tarmac and carries on out the far side — so the two dirt roads meeting the seal lie exactly opposite each other and what the map shows is a CROSSROADS, one road passing over another, rather than two junctions that happened to land near one another. R23 is not weakened by it, and the squareness is why: what R23 forbids is two roads SHARING GROUND, and a gravel road dragged over a sealed one at a slant shares it for as far as the slant runs, while a square crossing shares one PLACE and parts immediately — the two dirt arms are collinear, so the gravel is off the tarmac's mat within half a road width of the middle of it. Crossing at an angle is what would share ground, and it stays forbidden. The tarmac is the road that does not notice: full width, full surface, markings unbroken from one side to the other, and only the light smear of gravel a wheel carries across (`crossing.drag` — a fraction of a junction's, because a junction's is laid down by cars TURNING and nobody turns here). BOTH its arms are shut: a junction abandons one arm and the rally drives up the other, so a crossing is the one place on a stage where two barriers face each other across the road the car is on. And the tarmac STANDS PROUD — a public road is laid on a graded formation and a rally track is scraped along the field beside it, so the gravel climbs `crossing.ramp` metres of ramp onto the seal and drops off the far edge, which at stage speed is a jump. The step is the ROAD's, not a feature laid on the route: the platform is level along the public road and tilted at the RALLY's own grade, so the climb is `crossing.stand` on both sides of every crossing whatever the country was doing (read against a dead level plane it was two to three metres on a nominal one, and the ramps measured a third). The graded, paved area is a junction's own size; only the ELEVATION reaches out along the ramps, which keep their crown, their camber and their gravel — sized to the ramp instead, the platform was a sixty-metre plateau of bare earth with a face round its rim. One crossing per road, for R23's reason: both arms are cut from the tarmac and run to the edge of the map, so a route that met one road twice would leave two arms reaching into each other along the same piece of it.
- **R37** The country is LIVED IN. Every so often — far between, never two in sight of each other — a HOMESTEAD stands off the stage: a house on its own graded yard, a car or two outside it, and a dirt drive that comes down to the rally road and meets it SQUARE, the way a track off a farm meets the road it was built to reach. Squareness is what keeps R23 honest, exactly as it does for R36: a drive that ran alongside the stage would be a second carriageway on the stage's own shelf, a square one shares one place and parts at once. The drive is a real road — the ground flattens a shelf under it, the physics gives it gravel, the forest keeps off it, and it is laid to ARRIVE on its yard's level (its way is walked first, and its heights laid to that yard afterwards, at a minor road's grade and crest rule) rather than dropped onto it — and it is shut where it leaves the stage with whatever the marshals had on the lorry, for the same reason a branch is: a driver arriving at a fork must not have to wonder which way the rally goes. The house is on gravel of its own that the drive runs onto, and the trees along the drive are planted, not survived — a lane of them, on both sides, which is the one shape of forest a stage has that somebody put there on purpose. Nothing about a homestead may cost the route anything: it goes where a straight, dry, gently graded piece of country beside the road allows one, and where none does, there is no homestead. It never stands on another road (R17's tarmac or a branch), never in the water (R35), never beside a ford or a bridge (R18's channel has to be seen past the road's edge), never on the start's apron or inside the last stretch before the line, and its walls and its parked cars are as solid as they look. The numbers are `STAGE_RULES.homestead`: the stage is walked in `slot`-metre slots, each rolling against `spacing.mean` with `spacing.min` the floor between two; the drive is `drive.width` wide and `drive.length` long, leaving straight for `drive.straight` metres before it may wander at `drive.minRadius`; the yard is a disc of `yard.radius`, graded to the country's mean level across it and refused where the bare ground differs from that level by more than `yard.level` anywhere on it. SOME OF THEM ARE FARMS (`farms.ts`, in a country the biome says is farmed — `BiomeRules.farms`; `homestead.farm.chance` of the slots roll one, on the slot's own dice so a country that is not farmed draws the same houses in the same places): a farm's yard is the bigger disc a barn needs (`farm.yard.radius`), and across it from the house stands the BARN — a Swedish ladugård, longer, wider and a storey taller than the house (`farm.barn`, `drawBarnPlan`: two storeys of byre and hayloft under a gambrel or a steep gable, red boards nearly always, a loft ramp up one gable, ventilators on the ridge, half of them with a lean-to along the back), its front to the yard's middle and its back corners still on the pad, sometimes with a tower silo at its gable. Past the barn or behind the house lies a fenced PADDOCK with the stock in it — cattle more often than sheep, `paddock.head` of them, WHERE they may be is the engine's and where each one stands is the renderer's — and where the other site allows it a FIELD, ploughed, cut to stubble, or standing hay with the round bales across it. Each is a rectangle the country has to take: clear of every road and the water at its corners, its edges and its middle, and no more than `slope` out of level corner to corner, tried at the rolled size and then smaller (`farm.shrink`). The fence is a post every `postPitch` metres round the paddock with a gate in the side nearest the yard; the posts are solids of their own kind (`post`: wood, thin, a car goes through one), the barn's walls, the silo, the tractor and the trailer are as solid as a house and a car, the plough and the baler stand as one solid each, and the harrow lies under the ride-over bar. The paddock and the field are CLEARINGS to the terrain: the forest and the scatter keep off both (through `spurClearance`, the one channel everything that is not forest reads), and a ploughed field is turned soil to the wheels (`spurSurfaceAt` answers `sand`). The machinery stands where it was left — the tractor on the yard in front of the barn, the trailer at the gable, what the tractor pulls at the field's near edge.
- **R38** THE ROUTE NEVER RUNS FAR WITHOUT A CORNER IN IT. A stage is corners joined by straights, and the straight is the JOINT — long enough to change up through the box and pick a line into the next corner, never long enough to become somewhere the driver is going. The cap is `straightRun.max`, and the number behind it is a TIME rather than a distance: five seconds, which is as long as anyone wants to hold a wheel that is doing nothing. Metres come out of seconds through the speed the road is met at, so the cap is set by the SLOW case — a straight taken out of a corner the car had to stop for, where the first half of it is spent accelerating — and the fast case comes in well under it. It binds the RUN and not the segment: two straights drawn back to back are one straight to the person driving them, and stringing four of them together was how a stage came out with four hundred metres of nothing in the middle of it, every one of them legal on its own. A bend wider than `straightRun.bend` does not break the run either, because at rally pace that is a lean and not a corner. A BORROWED public road (R17) has its own, longer cap (`straightRun.borrowed`): the same five seconds, on a surface that is joined and held at speed. It is also what ENDS a borrow — a public road is laid to get somewhere and runs straight for two or three hundred metres at a time between its bends, so the rally leaves it where the road stops bending rather than where the `asphalt` dial stops asking, and the dial's ceiling is the country's rather than the dial's. `drive`'s `straight` check measures the result on the built stage, in seconds off the speed profile.
- **R39** TARMAC LEADS TO A TOWN. A public road (R17) was laid to reach somewhere, and every so often the somewhere is on the stage: a small town of ten to twenty buildings standing along the sealed road, on both sides of it, each on its own graded lot with its front to the street. It stands on the piece of tarmac the rally BORROWS — so the stage runs straight through it between the walls — or, where the stage only meets the road at a junction or a crossing, along the arm the tape shuts, so the road the rally does not take can be seen going somewhere; the arm the route leaves the road at is preferred, because it runs on ahead of the driver. A town is a VILLAGE, not a row of farms: most of it is houses, but it has what a farm has not — a block of flats, a grocery, the post office, a workshop — and the shops stand in the middle of it, placed by how far along the street the rows have reached as well as by count — and where the country's refusals still push a shop into an end quarter of the village as built, the street is walked again on fresh dice, twice, and a street that will not stand its shops in the middle on three tries stands none. The lots keep off the road's own verge (R31's bench is still the bench — a lot is graded level with the street's edge, and inside the bench that is the ground already), off every other road (R23) and every junction's own ground (`junction.parting`), out of the water (R35), and clear of the homesteads (R37), which keep clear of them; past the bench the country may stand no more than `town.lot.level` above a lot (the cone's bank behind it) or fall no more than that below it. The walls and the cars outside are as solid as they look. One town on a stage — a town is a place, and two in five kilometres is a suburb. The numbers are `STAGE_RULES.town`: `size` is the band, `street.min` the least sealed road a town fits on and `street.reach` how far down an arm it may stand, `lot` the front yards, the gaps, the pad and its blend, `kinds` how likely each kind is and the most of each, and `cars` how many stand outside each. `roads.town` and `roads.lots` measure the result: a borrowed run over `townRun` with no town on it, and every lot's front wall against its street's verge.
- **R40** A STAGE IS BUILT IN A COUNTRY, and the country is a DIAL. The biome (`knobs.biome`, `biomes.ts`) says what the land is made of and how it stands, whether there is water in it, what grows on it and in what company, and what the sky over it can do — as rows the generator reads, never as a branch in it. The taiga is the country every other rule was written against, and every multiplier in its row is 1. The desert has no water at all: no groundwater that surfaces (its table sits `ARID_TABLE` metres under the lake table everywhere), no basin that fills, no pit, no crossing on the route and no river traced through one; its hollows flatten into PANS instead (a soft floor `BiomeLand.floor` metres over the table, so a road dipping through one never reaches the "shore"), its relief is worn to `BiomeLand.relief` of the taiga's and its ranges to `mountains` of them, and the wind has piled its sand into DUNE FIELDS — a ridged noise stretched along one bearing per stage, lying on the rock as SOIL (a cactus roots in it, and it buries the stone) — that the road rides as a run of crests. Its bladed roads are SAND, a surface of its own to the physics (`TUNING.surfaces.sand`) and a bladed road like gravel to everything about the road's shape (`isLoose`). A biome never switches a rule off — a desert stage still obeys every one above — it moves what the rules draw from, exactly as the other dials do. `ground`'s `water`, `forest`, `relief` and `swamp` checks are held to the country's own bands (`ANALYSIS.ground.country`): a drop of standing water on a desert stage is a defect, and so is a taiga with none.
- **R41** THE RAILWAY IS LAID BEFORE THE STAGE IS, like the tarmac (R17), and the rally goes OVER it on a ramp. A country that carries one (`BiomeRules.railway`; `rail.chance` of its seeds) has a single track laid across the map edge to edge on nothing but the seed and the bare country (`layRailways`, walked by `layOne` on a railway's own `RAILWAY` numbers: radii of half a kilometre, a bend redrawn every few hundred metres, the water gone round on a branch line's curve), set out from the same side of the country as the public road so the two never cross, and held off it by R23's keep. It is a `Highway` of kind `rail` in the same network, so every clearance the search keeps from a road holds against the line for free; the route plans round it exactly as it plans round tarmac, may never borrow it or join it (`borrow.ts`, the town placer and the junction search ask the network for ROADS only), and may cross it once, SQUARE, by R36's own solve on the railway's own numbers (`rail.clear`, `seek`, `meet`, `look`). The crossing is a JUMP the organisers built: the straight over the line carries a `jump` feature whose ramp climbs `rail.lip.height` metres over `rail.lip.ramp` of road to a lip `rail.gap` short of the rails — the rails being where the compiled straight actually meets the line, read off its own geometry, since the plan solves the crossing on ideal arcs and the walk lands metres from them — and the road on the far side is at grade with the rails laid flush in it — so a car arriving at pace flies over the line, and a car that arrives slow drops onto the rails. What it flies over is the TRAIN: a timetable drawn per crossing (`drawSchedule`) runs one through around the time a driver at `rail.train.pace` reaches the crossing and every `period` after, each way in turn, at line speed, within `reach` of the crossing (it comes out of the fog and goes back into it; the period is held over the reach's transit so a single line never carries two trains pointing at each other). The train is a run of `railcar` solids the engine stands on the rails wherever the train is at this second (`trainSolidsNear`, a pure function of the stage clock — nothing about it is state), as tall as a wagon and MOVING at the train's speed: the contact model closes on the relative velocity, so a car standing on the rails is hit by the wagon rather than merely stopped by it, and a car in the air above a wagon's roof is not touched. Both arms of the line are cut to the edge of the map (`cutSpur`, flagged `rail`, ballast to the physics) and joined into the one walk the train runs; neither is shut, because nobody drives a railway, and the crossing is signed rather than taped. `roads.cross` measures the gravel against the line as it does against the tarmac, exempting the crossing over `crossingParting(width, "rail")`; `jumps.train` flies the ramp at the profile's speed and asks how much air is under the car over the rails against `RAILCAR.height`.
- **R42** THE CROWD DROVE HERE. Every stand (R27) is reached from a CAR PARK: a disc of graded gravel (`carPark.pad`, on a plane no steeper than `maxGrade`, fitted to the ground the terrain has already cut beside the road) with two rows of bays marked across it either side of one aisle (`carPark.bays` — at least `count.min` of them, so that with `empty` of them standing empty at least twenty cars are on it) and a car nosed into each, off a road that is NOT the rally road. The pad is planned BACKWARDS from the stand, the way a marshal plans it: the country round the stage is read as a coarse map (`carpark-map.ts`) of which cells a road may be driven through (R23's clearance from the route and every other road, dry ground) and which a person may walk across (off the route's corridor, out of the water, off a guard's mound); the crowd's walk from the stand's back (`carPark.walk`) is searched over it, and the first place along that walk the country will take a pad is the pad. The lane in leaves an abandoned arm past its barrier (R17, R36) square, `road.approach` metres from the pad, where one is within `reach`; otherwise it is searched out over the same map from the pad, into a road already there — an arm, or an earlier car park's own lane, which is one road across the country instead of two side by side — or out past the edge of the map (`SPUR.escape`), laid at a road's radius, following the country at a minor road's grade and crest rule (`followStep`, R34) and held inside the stage's cone (R31) exactly as a branch is, and refused where a step of it would stand where a road may not. Either way the lane is laid to ARRIVE: it leaves the pad on the pad's own plane and holds it a short run past the rim, or climbs onto the plane from the road's lip with the pad's level settled there, within what the run to the rim can make up at a road's grade (`road.rim` is the least such run); it closes onto the road it joins from as far out as the height between them needs; and a lane the country leaves standing over its join, thrown by the cone's floor, or blended into a ramp steeper than a road at the pad's rim is not built (`road:join-height`, `road:band`, `road:ramp` in the tally) — the search tries another way in. From the pad's rim a TRAIL is trodden to the back of each stand the walk reaches — threaded along the map's cells, steered round the real ground step by step, never inside the route's corridor and never through the water — with an arrow board beside it every `sign.pitch` metres. A stand that walks to a pad another stand already has is served by that one; a stand the country leaves no room to serve stays unserved, which `roads.served` reports against the share the country is allowed to refuse (a pocket the stage has folded shut has no way out that does not cross the route, and a hillside no pad fits on has nowhere to park). Nothing about it costs the route anything, and the cars are as solid as they look. On a streamed stage a stand's car park is decided `hold` metres after the stand, so every stand the same pad could serve exists by then and a chunked stream places the same car parks as one call. `roads.parking` measures every car park: its cars, its pad's clearance and grade, its lane's two ends, and its trails' length and their distance from the road.
- **R43** THE COUNTRY MAKES POWER. A stage in a modern country (`BiomeRules.energy` — the taiga's; the desert has no grid to feed) runs past the two things a hillside has on it now. A WIND FARM (`energy.wind`) is a string of `count` turbines, one make of machine per string (`hub` metres to the nacelle, `rotor` metres across — two hundred to the blade tip, an order of magnitude over anything else on the stage, in view from the moment the road comes within the fog's reach), placed against the BARE country: the stage is walked in slots that roll against `spacing.mean`, and a slot that rolls probes a fan of bearings out from the road across the `offset` band for the highest dry, clear ground; that ground has to be a `rise` over the road, or there is no wind farm here. The rest of the string follows the road's own bearing (within `swing`, so every tower stays in the band the road sees it from) at a `pitch` apart, each tower walked within `seek` of its slot onto the highest ground there, one way from the anchor and then the other when the country runs out. Every foot keeps `clear.route` off the route's centerline (a rotor's radius plus the corridor with room to spare), `clear.road` off every other road, `clear.settled` off every yard and lot, `apart` from every other string, and stands on a crane PAD of graded gravel (`pad`) the terrain flattens to the country's own mean level across it, inside R31's cone. A SOLAR FARM (`energy.solar`) is a rectangle in a WORLD-FIXED frame: its rows face the sun's azimuth (`facing` — `pwa/src/game/sky.ts`'s `SUN_AZIMUTH`, and a test holds the two together) whichever way the road runs, so a driver learns which way is south. Its size is rolled from `sizes` (a paddock's worth half the time, a field of them one time in five), tried on both sides of the road at three gaps from the corridor and shrunk by `shrink` when the country refuses; it is pushed out from the road by the corridor, the gap and exactly its own support in that direction, and it fits where a lattice of probes across it is clear of every road, the water and the settled places and the ground is within `slope` of level over its diagonal. Inside the fence stand `rows` of tables `row.pitch` apart, each `row.table` metres long, with an inverter cabin in the margin beside the gate, which is in the side nearest the road. The towers are rings of wall bays `hub` metres tall to the physics, the tables are runs of wall bays and the fence is posts; a solar farm is a clearing the forest and the scatter keep off; and the watercourses (R18) steer round a fence and a pad exactly as they steer round a road. Neither costs the route anything, and the renderer's only decision is where the blades are this frame: they turn in the game's own wind and the nacelles yaw to face into it. `roads.energy` measures every plant.
- **R44** THE COUNTRY DOES NOT STOP FOR THE RALLY. The road the stage borrows is closed, but the public roads it abandons at every junction (R17, R36) and the lanes into the car parks (R42) still carry whoever has somewhere to be, and a driver who leaves the stage meets them. The network of those roads is a FOREST (the tarmac lines never touch, R23; a lane hangs off one arm or off an earlier lane), so every journey has exactly one way to go: `traffic.ts` cuts the arms (from `pastBlock` metres beyond the barrier — a motorist turned back at the tape waits outside it) and the lanes at every PLACE they reach — the edge of the map, a car park's pad, a village on an arm, the tape across the closed road (`block`, or `town` when a town on the route stands within `townReach` of the junction) — and plans one route per ordered pair of places, walked on the driver's right of the centerline (a quarter of the road's width over) and resampled every `step` metres. Every KIND of journey (to the car parks from the edge of the map, from town out to the edge of it, and so on) gets an equal share of the traffic however many routes make it; car park to car park a third of one. Every route carries a posted LIMIT (`TRAFFIC_LIMITS`, km/h): an open road's, a village street's between `townMargin` either side of the lots, a car park lane's. The SPEED LIMIT SIGNS are stood by the same pass — one where the limit changes along a direction of travel (`sign.after` metres in), repeated every `sign.every`, `sign.out` past the road's edge on the driver's right, facing the traffic they apply to; a lane is signed on the way in only. What DRIVES the routes is the game's (`engine/game/traffic.ts`, `TRAFFIC` in `defs/traffic.ts`): a fleet sized by kilometres of lane, twenty vehicle models with real footprints and masses, each a coarse motorist — the limit, the bend, the gap to whatever is ahead — carrying a real `CarState` so the player can hit one through the same two-body contact the rivals use.

## The dials

Six knobs (`STAGE_DIALS` in the pre-race menu, `StageKnobs` in the engine), each `0..1`, decide what KIND of stage a seed builds — and a seventh, the COUNTRY (`knobs.biome`, R40), is a name rather than a number: which set of ranges the other six are read against. They never break a rule: they move the ranges the rules draw from, and the middle of every dial is the stage this generator built before they existed.

| Country  | What it is                                                                                                                                           |
| -------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `taiga`  | The boreal forest: spruce and pine on glaciated rock, lakes in the hollows, rivers under the fords, gravel roads. The default.                       |
| `desert` | Saguaro and creosote on the gravel fans, Joshua trees on the high ground, dune fields and salt pans, no water anywhere, and roads bladed out of sand |

| Dial        | 0                      | 1                                                                       |
| ----------- | ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `elevation` | a plain                | mountain country — the road's own relief and the landscape's, together  |
| `steepness` | worn: long open slopes | sheer: sharp crests, cliff-like fault steps, and rock cut through (R34) |
| `water`     | dry: the odd ford      | lakeland: wide rivers, concrete bridges, water in the nature            |
| `trees`     | open heath             | closed forest (the SOLID trunk field the car crashes into)              |
| `asphalt`   | all gravel             | all sealed — the share of the stage that is tarmac (R15)                |
| `width`     | a narrow lane          | a broad boulevard with room to place the car sideways (R21)             |

`elevation` and `steepness` are the same relief asked two different ways, and they are worth keeping apart: one says how HIGH the country stands, the other at what ANGLE it holds it. Alpine hills on a worn dial are long slopes you can see clean across; the same hills on a sheer one are rock either side of the road. `steepness` also decides how hard a face a road is cut through where it has to force a shoulder rather than go round (R34).

They reach every entry point: `compileStage(seed, length, knobs)`, `createGame({ knobs })`, `simulateStage({ knobs })`, `npm run sim -- --asphalt 0.8`, `npm run track -- --steepness 1`, `npm run analyze -- --steepness 1`, `npm run track -- --biome desert`, and the app's URL params (`?elevation=&steepness=&water=&trees=&asphalt=&width=&biome=`) — which is also how Roam builds the country it opens on. A track carries the dials it was built with (`track.knobs`), so the terrain field and the renderer shape themselves from the same set without being handed it separately.

## Stage lengths

The pre-race menu picks a length; each finite one maps to a band in `STAGE_RULES.stageLengths`, sized from the sim's measured bot pace so the minutes come out roughly true, with a world that grows with the band:

| Short      | ~1 min  | 1.45–1.8 km  |

| Extra long | ~7 min  | 10.4–11.8 km |

So a medium circuit is three laps of ~1.5–1.7 km, and it takes about the three minutes a medium sprint does.

## The endless stage

`compileStage(seed, "endless")` builds the opening stretch and hands back a track that extends itself: the engine's `step()` keeps `STAGE_RULES.endless.horizon` meters of road materialized ahead of the car, and the renderer streams world chunks and terrain tiles in around it (and drops them behind). Two mechanisms replace the finite search's whole-attempt reject:

- **A course.** The stream is point-to-point: it follows a slowly drifting bearing and keeps the road's heading within an error budget of it, so an endless run reads as a journey rather than a scribble — and the walk never curls back into its own tail.
- **A commit lag.** The search runs `endless.commitLag` meters ahead of the road it hands out; inside that lag it may still backtrack out of a pocket. The freeze boundary follows the generation high-water mark, so what a seed produces is independent of how the extends are chunked — the same seed always streams the same road.

## The circuit

`compileStage(seed, length, knobs, "circuit")` builds a stage that comes back to where it started. The search is the sprint's, with one thing added at each end:

- **A ring course.** Instead of the finite search's turn-back-at-the-boundary, the line follows a bearing that turns once through a full circle over the target lap length (a ring of radius `lap / 2π`), and a drawn turn whose exit strays past the error budget is redrawn. That is what makes the line go ROUND rather than wander; the budget is wide enough that what comes out is a lap and not a roundabout.
- **A solved closure.** Wherever a closure could still land inside the lap band, the search tries to solve a turn-straight-turn (a Dubins CSC path) from where the road stands back onto the grid's own pose. Both corners are drawn from the turn vocabulary's own radii, and the sweep each one takes has to fall inside that severity's angle band (R3) — so a closing corner is a corner this generator would have drawn anyway. The straight between them is where a circuit gets its MAIN straight.

The solve is exact on ideal arcs, but the compiler walks an arc in 2 m steps and lands a little off the circle it was drawn from. So the closure is solved against the road AS BUILT: solve for a goal, walk what that produced the way the compiler will, move the goal by whatever the walk missed by, repeat. Headings need no such treatment (a segment's total turn is its curvature times its length however finely it is stepped), which leaves two numbers converging in a handful of passes. The result closes to within a few centimetres — well inside one sample, so the road runs into its own grid with no seam to see or drive over.

An attempt that boxes itself in is abandoned quickly and restarted on a derived sub-seed rather than unpicked at length: most of the cost is walking the same ground twice, so many short attempts beat a few long ones. A circuit compiles in the same handful of milliseconds a sprint does.

`track.circuit` says which shape a compiled stage is, and the run does the rest: `createGame({ shape, laps })` races a circuit over `STAGE_RULES.circuit.laps` by default, crossing the line books a `lap` event and sends progress back to the grid, and only the last crossing finishes. A crossing that still owes a split board books neither (R28).

The seam is the thing to know about a lapped road: its first sample and its last are the same piece of gravel, so the nearest-sample search is free to answer with either, and the car stands on that seam twice a lap. Progress only ever creeps forward, so on a circuit a jump of more than half a lap ahead is read as the ROAD wrapping rather than the run advancing, and ignored (`engine/game/step.ts`) — without that rule, progress pins itself at the length of the road for the whole of every lap after the first, and the pacenotes, the crowd, the map gauge and the bot's own plan all read a car that has already finished.

## Pacenotes

The compiler emits one co-driver call per turn — contiguous same-direction turns merge into a single call — carrying direction, the tightest severity in the run, and the summed angle (`track.pacenotes`). The HUD reads them ahead of the car and shows the rally-style calls (EASY/MEDIUM/HARD LEFT/RIGHT, LONG past ~100°) — the corner being driven, plus the one after it drawn faint underneath when it lands inside the same lead. How far off the corner is, is the call's OPACITY rather than a printed number of metres: it comes up faint at the far end of the co-driver's lead and hardens to solid as the braking point arrives (never dimmer, however far out, than the next-corner plate behind it). The engine's positive direction reads as a LEFT turn on screen (the rendered world mirrors the engine's map view — the same one-flip rule as steering).

The sign beside the words is the CORNER, not a canned arrow per severity: `pwa/src/game/pace-shape.ts` walks the compiled centerline from the note's entry to its exit, squares the plan up on the entry so the road always arrives from the bottom of the box, smooths and resamples it, and fits it to the icon — so a double apex, a corner that tightens on the exit and a constant-radius sweep are three different pictures under one word. A corner past ~195° stops being drawn where it stops being one corner, and a note the compiled samples do not reach yet (an endless stage's streaming frontier) falls back to an ideal arc of its own angle. The PLATE points too: it is cut to a point on the side the corner turns toward and that point is filled solid in the severity's colour, so the direction reads before anything on the sign has been.

## Checkpoint placement (R28)

The boards are placed in the compiler's own segment walk
(`engine/mapgen/compile.ts`), from `STAGE_RULES.checkpoint`:

| Knob           | What it decides                                                                        |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `spacing`      | the target gap between boards, in SECONDS of driving — 15 s, because a split is a time |
| `pace`         | ...at this pace (m/s), the same measured bot pace the length bands are sized from      |
| `early` `late` | fractions of that gap at which the severity bar drops                                  |
| `runOut`       | how far past the corner's exit the board stands, capped by the road that carries it    |
| `finishClear`  | how much road before the finish gate no board may stand in                             |
| `gate`         | how far outside the road's own edge the board's line still counts a car through        |

A candidate is judged at the START of the segment AFTER a corner, because
only the next segment says whether the corner is over: a turn carrying
straight on in the same direction is one corner still happening, and a board
in the middle of a combination marks nothing. That makes the candidate the
whole co-driver call (`openNote`), with the combination's hardest severity.

The bar then relaxes with distance, which is the "prefer tight corners"
rule stated as an algorithm:

| Road since the last board | What earns a board |
| ------------------------- | ------------------ |
| under `early` × gap       | nothing            |
| `early` × gap to the gap  | a HARD corner      |
| the gap to `late` × gap   | hard or medium     |
| past `late` × gap         | any turn at all    |

In practice that puts a board after a medium or hard corner more than nine
times in ten, at a mean gap a little over the target — a stage would rather
carry an extra hundred metres than mark a bend nobody had to think about.
`make track` draws them on the schematic, which is how the placement is
judged.

An endless stage places them the same way as it streams; a circuit's boards
belong to the LAP and are driven through again on each one.

## Kerb placement (R26)

Kerbing is placed for a reason, and there are only four of them. A stage edged in stripes from end to end is a bobsleigh run with trees
behind it; kerbing at the corners that earn it is a road with a rally on it,
and it is also readable — a driver at 160 km/h has about a second to take in
what the road is telling them, so the marking has to mean something.

`engine/mapgen/kerbs.ts` decides WHERE — arc-span zones per side, and the
list of MARKERS those zones become; `pwa/src/game/kerbs.ts` decides what one
looks like. The placement is engine-side because one of the markers is
solid: the contact model collides the same list the renderer draws, so the
slab that throws a cut apex is a slab the player could see coming.

| Role       | Where it goes                                                      | What it says                                                                                      |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **apex**   | the INSIDE of the bend, around its tightest point                  | "Aim here." The target that defines the line, and what stops it being cut into the ditch.         |
| **exit**   | the OUTSIDE, through the last of the bend, ending where it does    | "The road ends here." Centrifugal force takes the car wide under power; this is the usable width. |
| **entry**  | the OUTSIDE, through the first of a hard corner                    | "Brake." The turn-in board — so it only appears on a corner that needs braking for.               |
| **hazard** | both sides, wrapped around a bridge deck or a jump lip's shoulders | "This one will hurt." High-risk collision zones, marked all the way round.                        |

**A corner zone never leaves its corner.** Marking is what a BEND wears, so
every span is clipped to the arc its pacenote covers: `exitRun` and `entryRun`
are ceilings a short corner simply does not fill, not runs of road the marking
is guaranteed. A run of posts standing down an empty straight is the
end-to-end stripe with gaps in it — from the car it announces a corner forty
metres before there is one, and from above it is a road wearing kerbing at
random. Only a hazard reaches past a bend, because what it is wrapped around
is not one.

Corners under `kerb.minAngle` (~41°) get nothing at all, which is what makes
kerbing an event: on a stage of soft sweepers almost nothing is marked, and
the one hairpin reads from a long way out. Only corners past
`kerb.entryAngle` also earn a turn-in board.

**What is built there depends on the surface.** Tarmac gets a low-profile
continuous kerb running through the whole zone. Gravel does not: a poured
concrete kerb down the side of a forest road is a lie, so a dirt stage is
marked with a run of orange-and-white marker posts at `kerb.postSpacing`, and
anti-cut blocks at an apex (`kerb.blockSpacing`) where cutting is the
temptation. Discrete objects with country between them, never a painted band.

Orange rather than the rally red the real thing is painted in, and the reason
is what the colour SAYS at speed: red is this game's colour for something
having gone wrong — the tape across a closed branch, the call that says a
part has broken, the marker on the map — so a corner lined in it reads as a
reprimand for a line the player has not even taken yet.

**A post and a block part company at the contact model.** A post stops
nothing and never reaches the physics: the renderer knocks it flat off the
same body box the engine would have used, like a marshal's cone. A block is
SOLID, and the one piece of scenery in the game the car may hit with nothing
breaking — it costs speed, rolls the body, shoves the car back out of the
inside of the corner and thuds, and it never folds a panel
(`TUNING.collision.kerb`, resolved by `clipKerbs`). Cutting an apex has to be
paid for, not punished with the run.

**A block is BEDDED IN, and priced off what climbing it costs.** Only
`KERB_MARKER.block.proud` of the slab's thickness stands above the verge;
the rest is buried, and the renderer sinks it by exactly the same amount.
That is what makes it a lump the wheels ride over rather than a step they
cannot climb — and it is why everything the contact does is priced off a
BITE rather than the closing speed. A slab a hand high cannot take a car's
whole road speed off it however fast the car arrives, and when it could,
an apex lined with them was a wall: the car came out of the row at a
fifteenth of the speed it went in at. `drive`'s `kerb` check is the
instrument — it drives the reference car down every apex row on the stage
through the real contact model and reports the share of its speed it loses,
which should be about a fifth: a price a driver pays on purpose to
straighten a corner.

## Rolling elevation

Generated stages are genuinely 3D, and their height is TWO things (R34). Under everything is the LANDSCAPE THE ROAD FOLLOWS: the bare country's own height along the route, put through a lag and clamped in gradient and in vertical curvature (`STAGE_RULES.elevation.follow`), which is what a road builder does and what puts a stage down the valleys instead of at an altitude of its own. Where the country is gentler than the clamps the road simply follows it; where it is steeper the difference is cut and fill, and that difference is where R34's rock faces come from. The road refuses to follow the country under water — a lake is crossed on an embankment, never along the bed of one.

On top of that rides the road's own ROLL: `compileStage` sums octaves of seeded value NOISE along arc length (`STAGE_RULES.elevation`), each octave half as long and a fraction as tall as the one above it. Noise rather than sine waves because a sine repeats — every rise the same shape as the last — and octaves shorter than a few dozen meters are not hills at all but a washboard: on a road sampled every 2 m they flip the grade back and forth every ripple, which is what the eye actually sees. Per-stage character (how tall, how long, how rough) is drawn from the rule ranges, so one seed rolls and the next is nearly flat. Grades live on the straights and flatten through corners (`straightness` in `compile.ts`) — partly stage-design taste, partly load-bearing: a car cutting inside a turn sweeps whole samples of arc per physics step, and a real grade across that sweep would read as the ground falling away. Fords carve their own dips into the profile (R12): the water lies flat below the lowest surrounding grade, with the approach eased over an apron on each side. Synthetic tracks (`compileTrack(seed, segments)`) stay flat rigs so scripted physics tests measure exactly what they script.

## Determinism and the daily stage

`generateStage(seed, length)` → `compileStage(seed, length)` is deterministic end to end: the same seed always builds the same stage, on every device — endless streams included. The app seeds from the day number, so everyone drives the same stage today; each finish advances to the next seed. Seeds are also the bug-report currency (the HUD shows the stage seed).

## Looking at the output

```sh
make track                                  # previews/track-1..6.png (medium)
npm run track -- --seeds 42,99              # specific seeds
npm run track -- --length xlong             # a stage length band
npm run track -- --shape circuit            # a closed lap circuit (R22)
npm run track -- --length endless --km 8    # a streamed endless stretch
npm run track -- --zoom junctions           # one close-up per junction
npm run track -- --zoom junctions --span 45 # ...and how much country around it
```

and writes TWO pictures per seed:

- `track-<seed>.png` — the **schematic**: the route as a map. Surfaces (gravel / asphalt / deck / ford), jump lips, crests, the branches at each junction, and the corner guards, in colors that can be told apart at a glance while judging the rules.
- `track-<seed>-render.png` — the **place**: the shaded landscape with its lakes and rivers and forest, and the road drawn across its full width — wheel tracks, shoulders, markings, bridges, junctions, and the branches running off the map. The racing route is called out in magenta with direction arrows, because the stage and the roads it borrows are the same kind of road and at a whole stage's zoom they look it.

A whole stage's frame resolves a junction, a bridge or a guarded hairpin as a few dozen pixels — not enough to tell a built place from a collision of two ribbons. `--zoom junctions` re-frames the same renderer tightly around each junction the seed produced, which is the loop for working on them: change a number, re-render, LOOK.

The frame fits the road and lets the nature fill the rest, so a longer stage renders from further up. Dials pass straight through (`npm run track -- --elevation 1 --water 0.9`), which is the fastest way to see what one does. Pair with `make sim` (`--length` picks the band) — a rules change must keep bots finishing (see [simulation.md](simulation.md)).

## Describing one stage

`make track` is for judging the RULES across seeds. `make level` is for reasoning about ONE stage — a campaign level, or any seed on any dials — without driving it:

```sh
make level LEVEL=1                          # campaign stage 1 → previews/level-taiga-1.png + .txt
make level LEVEL=taiga-3                    # ...by id
make level LEVEL=1 FOCUS=J1                 # a close-up, 240 m across, around the first jump
make level LEVEL=1 FOCUS=T4 SPAN=160        # ...around a call, at 160 m across
make level LEVEL=1 FOCUS=1200               # ...around 1200 m along the stage
make level SEED=38 LENGTH=short             # any seed on the default dials
make level SEED=7 SHAPE=circuit ARGS='--asphalt 0.6 --water 0.9'
make level LEVEL=2 ARGS=--json              # the same features as data
make level ARGS=--list                      # the campaign's stages and their seeds
```

It loads the engine and the campaign's level table and nothing else — no build, no browser — and draws:

- the ground tinted by height with contours, and the lakes and streams in blue; the road at its width, coloured by surface (gravel, tarmac, bridge deck, ford), faded past the finish gate; the abandoned branches and the public roads in grey;
- every co-driver call as a stroke down the road in its severity's colour (green easy, amber medium, red hard), labelled `T3 HR` — the number it is called as, the severity's initial, and the driver's left or right;
- every jump lip (`J1`), blind crest (`CR1`), ford (`F1`), bridge (`B1`), split board (`CP1`), junction (`JN1`), the start and the finish;
- every railway crossing (`RX1`, R41): where the rally goes over the line, with the timetable — a train every so many seconds at line speed, the first at a stated second of the clock — and the ramp's lip listed as the jump it is;
- every homestead (`H1`, R37): the yard, the drive off the stage and the house's footprint, with its parked cars and lane trees among the solids;
- every town (`V1`, R39): each lot's pad and its building's footprint down both sides of the street it stands on, and which street that is — the borrowed run, or the arm at a junction;
- every car park (`P1`, R42): the pad and the cars on it, the lane in and what it comes off — an abandoned arm, another car park's lane, or the edge of the map — and the trails out to the stands it serves, with the boards along them;
- every wind farm (`W1`, R43): each turbine as its rotor's sweep, at true size, with the tower a white dot in it — with how many machines the string is, how tall, and how far its top pad stands over the road; and every solar farm (`PV1`): the fence in the glass's blue with the cabin in it, and how many rows of how many tables are inside;
- the trees, rocks and logs standing within 12 m of the road's edge, and the corner guards;
- the road's elevation profile against distance under the map, with the same ids on it, and the key down the side.

The map is drawn north up with EAST ON THE LEFT, which is the driver's hand: the engine's heading grows from north toward east and the game reads that as a LEFT turn, so a conventional east-right map would mirror every call.

The table it prints (and writes beside the picture) says what each id is, in stage order — for a call its severity, angle, length, tightest radius and the grade into it; for a jump its lip height, the ramp's grade and length, and which call it lands before; for each of them what is standing within reach of the edge and where the nearest thing is. The ids are the same on the picture, in the table and in `--json`, so "the first jump on level 1" means `J1` in all three.

Most checks are ceilings: less is better and zero is perfect. Eight are **bands**, and they are the ones that catch a stage for being too clean rather than too broken — how often the gravel has a bump in it (a road that measures zero is one nobody bladed or drove on), the road's nominal width, how much the rideable corridor pinches and opens out, how much the MAT's own width does, how much wider the bends are cut than the straights, how far over the gravel corners lie, how far the road travels up and down per kilometre, and what an apex cut over the anti-cut blocks costs (a block that takes nothing is no deterrent; one that takes everything is a wall somebody left in the road). A generator with only ceilings on it optimises toward a plane.

Sparseness is why the bump check counts EVENTS rather than averaging: a road with one heave in it and a road with a continuous ripple of the same energy average identically and are nothing alike.

The score is a compass, not a grade — read the FINDINGS. A run that scores 92
with an error in it needs the error fixed, not the score improved. The exit
code is non-zero on any error, so a change can be gated on it.

## Extending the vocabulary

New content kinds (say, tunnels or level crossings) follow the pattern: a feature enum value + placement rules in `rules.ts`, placement logic in `assignFeature`, geometry in `compile.ts`, an R-rule stated in prose in both files and this document, and an invariant test in `tests/mapgen_test.ts`. The renderer picks the feature up from the samples.
