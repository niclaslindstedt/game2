title: The cockpit has a real dashboard, dirty glass all round, and a mirror that looks back through the car
---

From the driver's seat the HUD's rev counter, gear and speed come down and the dashboard takes over: two dials with figures round them, a gear figure between them, a shift lamp on the binnacle, a row of tell-tales that light for a bulb check on the line, and the co-driver's tripmeter with the stage's distance and the distance since the last split board. The fascia has louvred vents, pilot lamps over the switches, stalks on the column, the intercom and the extinguisher pull on the tunnel, and a fire bottle in the co-driver's footwell.

The windscreen and the side windows now cake with the road the way the back window always did — the coat sits on the outside of the glass and was only ever drawn for cameras outside the car — and the side glass, which nothing wipes, closes in over a gravel stage. The cockpit's lens is narrower, so the screen fills the frame.

The rear-view mirror's camera stands on the mirror hanging in the windscreen and looks back through the cabin, in every view: the strip at the top of the frame and the cockpit's own mirror both show the back window, the dirt on it, and the road through what is left — reversed, the way a mirror is.
