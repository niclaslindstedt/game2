title: Snow that remembers what drove over it
---

Snow is now a surface the car CHANGES rather than one it merely drives on.
Its depth is real, and a wheel compresses it: what the car packs down stays
packed, so a run through untouched powder leaves a trail of two ruts with a
crown standing between them, and a second lap is driven on the first one's
tracks.

That trail is faster and looser than the snow beside it, which is the whole
game of a winter stage. What holds a car back in snow is the depth it has
to plough — a worked track has almost none left, so untouched powder holds
a car to a crawl where its own ruts let it run — but a polished track also
grips worse than the fresh snow either side of it, exactly as a real winter
road does. And a rut has walls: the ruts steer the car, and coming out of
one sideways means climbing a bank.

A stage's road arrives already worn into that pattern. There is a cover of
snow standing on it, deepest at the untouched edges, lower over the crown,
and worn down to a polished floor in the two tracks the traffic before the
rally left in it.

Snow also reads as snow now — it was rendering as a warm grey — and a bank
of it is displaced by the car rather than hit by it, so ploughing through
one never damages the shell.
