title: The trough a car ploughs in snow is drawn as deep as it really is
---

A car working its way through deep snow flattens it by a third of a metre and
more, and the snow was being drawn coming down by a couple of centimetres —
so the tracks were two thin lines and the broad path the car's body presses
was not visible at all.

Two different numbers had been confused. Packing a column of snow both
shortens it and firms it, so the wheels sink by only the loose share of what
was taken out while the top of the snow loses the whole of it. The world was
being drawn at the wheels' number. It is now drawn at the snow's own, which is
four to nine times larger: a car's track through the deepest fields is a
trough two thirds of a metre deep in the wheel lines with a broad pressed
floor between them, rather than a pair of scratches.
