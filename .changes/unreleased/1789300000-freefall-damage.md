title: A fall now costs the car what a fall costs a car
---

Everything bolted to the car — the arms, the drive shafts, the engine on
its mounts — is now rated for a load, and an arrival that pulls harder
than the rating tears it off. Nothing knows how far the car fell: a wall
met at 100 km/h is stopped over half a metre of crumple zone and the car
keeps all four wheels, while the same car arriving at the bottom of a
mountain asks the same arms for ten times the load and loses them. A car
that goes over an edge nose-down now lands on its bumper rather than its
floorpan, the floor folding crazes the glass the way the roof always did,
and the sump and the steering rack are on the list of things the ground
can find under a car. A 5 m drop is still free; a 50 m drop is the engine,
and a plunge off an alpine face puts all four wheels on the ground behind
the car and ends the run where it stops.
