title: Power lines across the landscape
---

The country that makes power now carries the grid that takes it away. A
400 kV transmission line — two lattice legs under one crossarm, three
bundled conductors and two earth wires on the peaks — is laid across the
whole map on a little under half the stages, and the rally passes under it
wherever it happens to.

It is surveyed rather than decorated: the towers ARE the line, so it turns
only at one, and where the next one stands is a clearance problem rather
than a spacing one. The conductor's sag goes as the square of its span, so
the next tower goes at the longest reach whose wire still clears the ground
under it — which puts the towers on the brows and the long spans, sagging
thirty metres, over the hollows. A tower stands off every road, out of the
water and on ground its legs can reach, on poured concrete footings; where
the line crosses forest, the trees are cut back to a wayleave that runs
away over the hills.
