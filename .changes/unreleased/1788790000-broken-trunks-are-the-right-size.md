title: A trunk you break is the size of the trunk that was there
---

Hit a fallen log and what came loose was a barrel five metres across — the
piece was being cut to the log's COLLISION CIRCLE, and a fallen log's circle
is the length it covers, not the thickness of the wood. Small trees had the
same fault from the other end: a tree's circle is its trunk plus the boughs
around it and never comes below a third of a metre however young the tree, so
a sapling dropped a mature trunk, and the log the car had just driven through
came away bigger than the tree beside it.

Every piece is now cut from the wood the forest actually draws. A snapped log
is the log; a young trunk is as slim as the tree it came off and an old one
the same shape three times over; a kicked stump is a stump rather than a
stump wearing a dunce's cap. A trunk that was already lying down comes loose
LYING DOWN, at the bearing it lay along and one bole-radius off the ground
instead of leaping half a trunk into the air first, and it keeps no branches
— a log that has lain there long enough to grow moss has none left.

The two fallen trunks are also drawn centred on the point they were planted
at now, so a log stops having a phantom half you could hit with nothing
there, and a visible half you could drive straight through.
