title: Four driven wheels now earn their keep
---

What a car can put down is the grip under it times the weight standing on the wheels that actually drive it, so the four-wheel drive pulls about twice what either two-wheel drive does — and because a climb pitches weight off the nose onto the tail and spends the tyres' budget just holding station, the front-driver now scrabbles up a steep loose hill, the rear-driver digs in, and the four-wheel drive simply goes up it.
