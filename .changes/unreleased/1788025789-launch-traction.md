title: The start line is a decision now — wait for the green, or spin them away
---

Sitting on the revs through the countdown no longer costs nothing. The clutch
comes out on whatever the engine was holding, the tyres light up instead of
gripping, and the car digs a hole where it should have left: a driver who
waited with the pedal up is 13–17 m up the road at five seconds, and still a
few metres ahead even after taking a third of a second to react to the lights.
Half a second, and it has all been given back — the start is worth a reaction
time and not a stage.

You can see it as well as feel it. The driven wheels are drawn spinning, the
needle flares with them, and both of them throw a cloud twice the size of a
clean getaway's for a second and a half. A four-wheel-drive can still be
floored off the line; a rear-driver never quite can.

And the car now trembles on its mounts while the engine is being worked — a
few millimetres on the grid and off the line, in the bodywork and from behind
the wheel, gone by the time there is any road speed to feel instead.
