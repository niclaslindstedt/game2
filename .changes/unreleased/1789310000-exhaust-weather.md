type: Changed
title: Exhaust that answers the weather and the throttle
---

The smoke off a tailpipe is now two substances instead of one grey cloud.
Most of what an engine puts out is water, and water is invisible until the
air is cold enough to condense it — so a summer stage's pipe is clean, and a
winter one hangs a pale plume off the back of every car on the line, thickest
in the first half-minute while the pipework is still cold steel. The other
half is soot, and that answers the DRIVER: a stab of throttle low down blows
a puff of black out of the back, a clutch dropped on a lit axle is the
blackest moment in a run, and lifting off clears the pipe entirely. A cold
stage washes the soot pale, so the same bootful is black in August and grey
in February.

The cloud itself is rebuilt on the same puff shader the tyre smoke uses —
every puff its own size, angle and opacity — so it billows instead of sliding
along as a row of sprites. Warm and off the throttle it now draws nothing at
all, and the DETAIL row's EXHAUST stop thins the cold-weather plume as well
as choosing whose pipes smoke.
