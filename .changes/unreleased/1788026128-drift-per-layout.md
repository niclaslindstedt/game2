title: Every car drifts less, and the front-driver has to be asked
---

The whole roster's committed drift is shallower, and where it sits is now the
difference between the three cars rather than a detail of them. The saloon —
the rear-driver — holds the angle the hatch used to; the hatch holds half of
that and washes wide instead, and the coupe sits between them.

What the hatch gets in exchange is a set of MOVES that actually work. Turn
into a tight corner on the throttle and it understeers, as a front-driven car
does. Lift and trail the brake into it and the nose comes down, the rear goes
light and the car rotates. Throw a Scandinavian flick at it and it hangs out
at nearly twice the angle the wheel alone could reach. Pull the handbrake and
it will get round a hairpin — the lever now works below the 70 km/h floor,
because the corners that need it are the slow ones.

The bots learned all of this too: they read the same limits the car does, so
a car that will not rotate is now driven on the brakes instead of thrown at
the corner and hoped for.
