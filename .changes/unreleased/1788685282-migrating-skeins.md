title: Geese and swans crossing the northern skies
---

The taiga and the Alps now have birds that are GOING somewhere. In spring
and autumn, skeins on passage — long vees of geese and straight trailing
lines of white whooper swans, high over the stage and heading north or
south depending on which end of the year it is. In summer the same birds
are still there, lower and in smaller loose groups, on their way to the
next lake rather than to another country.

They arrive rather than appear: each crossing is pitched over a point a few
hundred metres ahead and flies in from a kilometre back, and the wingbeat
runs down the formation as a wave instead of every bird striking together.
Both have voices of their own in the world's roster — the goose a reedy
nasal honk, four of them staggered and spread wide; the swan a longer,
purer bugle answered from the far side of the skein — raised often on
passage (geese go over at night, too) and rarely in summer.
