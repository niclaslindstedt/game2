type: Changed
title: The three cars now differ by GROUND, not by how far sideways they go
---

The rear-driver and the four-wheel-drive have been brought much closer to
the front-driver, which is the car the roster is calibrated on. A flick used
to put 48° into the saloon against the hatch's 29° in the same corner, so
one car flew and one was a fight; the layouts now sit within a stride of
each other on the wheel, the pedals and the lever alike.

What separates them instead is where they are quick and where they are FUN:

- **The saloon owns the tarmac.** A driven rear axle spins its tyres up
  against sealed grip and finds an angle the surface refuses everybody else,
  so it drifts a paved corner about as freely as the hatch drifts a gravel
  one — with the smoke and the tyres singing for it. The throttle still
  deepens the slide, and the tail still steps out at walking pace; both are
  just no longer arguments the driver has to win.
- **The works sedan owns the winter.** Four driven wheels each spend less of
  their friction budget on going forwards, which is worth nothing on gravel
  and a great deal on ice, packed snow and standing water.
- **The hatch is untouched**, on every surface and every technique.
