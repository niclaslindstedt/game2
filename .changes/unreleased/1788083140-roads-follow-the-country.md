type: Changed
title: Roads now follow the country, and cut through it where they must
---

Stages used to be laid at a height of their own invention — the road wandered
up to forty metres against the ground it crossed, and the terrain quietly
planed the landscape away to reach it. That is why the country beside a stage
read as a lawn you could see clean across.

The road is now laid ALONG the land: it runs down the valleys, climbs only
where it has to, and where the country is steeper than a road may be the
difference comes out as cut and fill. Where it is forced through a shoulder of
rock the face is left STANDING rather than battered back into a ramp — a
cutting, with the bedrock showing and nothing growing on it. Sealed roads get
the blasted walls; a gravel road is scraped in cheaply and mostly goes round.

A new TERRAIN dial (worn / rugged / sheer) says how steeply the country holds
its relief and how hard a face a road is cut through — separately from HILLS,
which says how high it stands. It is on the pre-race card, in Roam, and on the
URL as `?steepness=`.

The rival crews learned to read it: they now plan a corner off what is beside
it as well as what is under it, and lift where running wide would put them in
a lake instead of on the grass.
