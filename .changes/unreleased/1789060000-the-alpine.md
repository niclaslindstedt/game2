title: The Alps — a third country, down off the mountain through hairpins, tunnels and snow
---

Win the desert's table and the ALPS open: six more stages set in a high
mountain range that is nothing like the two countries before it. The
mountains come first here — a massif of ridges hundreds of metres over flat
valley floors with a turquoise lake in each, bright summer pasture above a
forest that stops at a treeline, grey rock and snow above that — and the road
is made to fit them. A stage starts on a shoulder beside the snow and comes
down: fast and open across the upper flank, then a stack of hairpins down a
face, through a tunnel where a spur is in the way, along a cliff road with
the lake far below, into the trees, and out onto the valley floor among the
chalets. Nothing keeps you on the road; the drop is the drop.

Above the snowline the road is SNOW, a surface of its own: barely half of
gravel's grip, a slide that runs further and settles later than on anything
else, and a soft landing when you get it wrong. Roam's PEAKS dial, offered
only in this country, runs from one lone mountain standing in a plain to a
whole range of them. There are no public roads to borrow up here: the
ASPHALT dial seals the route itself, tarmac from the valley up to a line and
the mountain's own grey gravel above it. The sky is thin and clear, with
choughs wheeling round the rock, and the weather comes down onto the flanks
rather than staying over them.
