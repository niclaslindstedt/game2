title: The desert's sand looks like sand
---

A desert stage's ground was a flat warm colour with nothing on it, so the
dunes it is actually built out of — twenty-odd metres of sand standing over
the trough beside them — read as bare rolling hills rather than as a sand
sea.

Sand now wears the wind: the ripple field the wind sorts it into, a hand's
width apart and running square across the breeze, and the broad sheen dry
sand carries toward the sun, which is what separates a lit windward face from
the slip face behind its own crest. Neither is on rock or on anything steep,
because the wind strips a face and leaves the sand on the flats, and both
fade out before they are finer than a pixel.
