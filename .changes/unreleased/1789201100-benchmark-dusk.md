title: The benchmark runs at dusk, so the lighting row costs something
---

LIGHTING's expensive half is the BEAMS — spotlights every lit pixel in the
world pays for — and a car at noon has none of them on. So on a midday
stage the whole ladder from one headlamp to four cost the same nothing, and
the row read as dead for the same reason DISTANCE did.

The benchmark now pins its own clock at 18:30 rather than taking the
stage's. The lamps come on when the sun drops under four degrees; that puts
it at 3.4 — lit lamps on a world still lit from the side, where a night
stage would have taken away the very geometry the other rows are there to
measure.

One minute of racing is one hour of sun, so the run is a band and not a
point: warm-up to last frame carries the sun from about +2.8° to about
−3.4°, across the horizon and no further. Every frame has its lamps lit and
none of it is night, which is what keeps the workload one shape from the
green to the end.
