type: Changed
title: Spinning wheels — and a needle that flares with them
---

The driven wheels now turn at the revs through the gear they are in, so a
lit-up axle visibly outruns the road: the rear-driver's back wheels race
away from a standstill, and every car's driven pair spins up again mid-drift,
where a tyre spending its grip sideways has less of it left to drive with.
The tachometer and the engine note flare with them, because the needle is
those wheels — while the shift light stays on road speed, since a car
spinning its wheels in second wants the throttle backed off, not third.

A wheel can never spin faster than the engine can turn it: a fully lit axle
winds to the top of the gear it is in and no further, so first gear lights up
off the line and top gear cannot spin at all.
