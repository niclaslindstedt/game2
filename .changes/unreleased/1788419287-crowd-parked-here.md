title: The crowd stands where a crowd could have got to
---

Spectators now gather on the INSIDE of a corner, which is where a rally
marshal actually puts them — a car that lets go leaves at a tangent and
finishes on the outside. They stand only in corners worth the walk in, and
only where the country behind them will carry a car park: a corner with
nowhere to have parked gets nobody.

Where they parked has been rebuilt to match. A car park is a patch of bladed
gravel in the grass a few hundred metres off the course, with the crowd's own
cars on it — a carful of people each, so eight spectators at a corner is
three cars behind it rather than the two dozen that used to be nosed into
painted bays. And the country now carries the tarmac it was only ever
planning: a public road the rally never meets is built along its own line,
rim to rim, which is a road the lane out of a car park can join.
