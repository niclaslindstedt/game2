title: The desert's cacti are cacti again — every plant in the country rebuilt, and six new ones beside them
---

The desert was being carried by the wrong plant. What broke its horizon was
a dense flat-topped umbrella on a smooth green trunk — a savanna acacia by
any honest reading, and the wrong continent entirely — while the cacti
behind it were lengths of painted pipe: smooth cylinders with square-elbowed
arms, a barrel cactus that was a paint tin with a sandy lid, and a prickly
pear whose pads were lumps of rock.

Every one of them has been rebuilt from the shape it actually has.

A cactus is FLUTED, and that is most of what the rebuild is. Its column is
an accordion of ribs that swell and shrink with the water it is holding, and
under a low sun those ribs are a run of vertical shading bands down the
whole plant. The saguaros, the organ pipes, the barrels and the hedgehogs
are now built on a ribbed column rather than a cylinder, and a saguaro's arm
leaves the trunk sideways and turns through a real ELBOW before it runs up —
the single most recognisable shape in the American desert, and the one the
old right angle threw away.

A desert tree is not a savanna tree either. The wash trees grow on several
thin stems out of one root and carry so little foliage that you can read the
sky through them, so the palo verde, the mesquite and the new ironwood are
open scatters at half a dozen different heights rather than one solid plate.
The palo verde keeps its GREEN BARK, which is the whole point of the plant,
and blooms yellow in its crown alone rather than turning the trunk yellow
too.

Also rebuilt: the teddy bear cholla, now a dense pale fuzzy club instead of
an antler; the ocotillo, a fountain of straight woody whips rather than a
bundle of sticks with blobs on the end; the Joshua tree, thatched in the
dead leaves it never sheds and ending every limb in a fist of dark daggers
over a skirt of more of them; the yucca's cream panicle, which used to read
as a bulrush a thousand miles off its bog; and the creosote, which you can
now see straight through, as a creosote flat requires.

Six plants joined them: the organ pipe cactus, the desert ironwood, the
hedgehog cactus and its April magenta, the chain-fruit cholla with its
hanging chains, the century plant's six-metre flowering mast, and — most
importantly — the triangle-leaf bursage, the commonest plant in the Sonoran
desert and the nurse plant most of the saguaros standing over it started
their first fifty years under. Its grey domes are now the ground layer
everywhere, which is what the country was missing more than any cactus.

And every green in the paint box was pulled toward grey. A cactus's skin is
a dull, slightly blue grey-green under a waxy bloom, and the distance
between that and a tree's leaf green is most of what tells you at speed that
you are looking at a desert.
