title: A car ploughing deep snow leaves a trench, not a scuff
---

The trail a car leaves in snow is drawn with banks of thrown snow either
side of it, sized by how far the wheels actually pressed the ground down —
but the height of those banks was capped at ten centimetres, which was
sized back when the deepest blanket in the game was half a metre. Now that a
permanent snowfield lies metres deep, a car ploughing through one was still
leaving a ten-centimetre scuff on it.

The cap is now the deepest rut the snow can physically give, so the trail is
sized by the snow rather than by the cap: on the deepest field the bank
either side goes from sixteen centimetres to seventy. A winter road, which
has almost nothing left to give, draws exactly the trail it always did.
