title: Falls are aerodynamic now, and the speedo counts them
---

Drive off a cliff and the car keeps gathering speed the way it should. The
air is modelled properly once the wheels are off the ground: drag acts on the
whole velocity rather than just the forward speed, so a fall accelerates hard
and then settles at a real terminal velocity — past 180 km/h inside three
seconds and topping out well beyond anything the road will give.

How fast depends on which way the car is falling and on the car itself. A
nose-first dive is a dart; going off flat is a sheet of plywood. A rear wing
is the one part whose whole job is the air: it makes downforce over a jump and
carries the nose several degrees high, and in a long fall the same blade works
the other way and pitches the car onto its nose.

The speedometer now reads the car's speed through space, vertical included, so
a fall reads as the fastest thing in the run instead of the needle dropping
toward zero. On the road nothing changes — the two are the same number there.
