type: Changed
title: The time trial's results card is a high score board again
---

Finishing a time trial used to put up a card with a total time on the left
and a bare list of initials floating in the middle of a very wide, very
empty banner. It is now the same card the campaign ends on: where the run
landed on the stage's board is the place, in the numerals a race's place is
written in; under it the total time, and under that the margin to the row
above — or, off the ten, the row that has to be beaten to get on at all.

The board itself is a table rather than a list, with a photograph of the car
that set each time, the difficulty it was set at and the day it was set. A
plate on each car says which gearbox drove it: AT or MT. Those three are on
the row because they are what a time on this board actually costs — a
different car, a different box and a different setting are three different
achievements, and a board that hides them cannot be argued with. The top
three places wear gold, silver and bronze: a struck medal for the rank, the
metal around the row, and more height than the seven places still going.

**The three letters are typed onto the board.** The naming card is gone.
When a run makes the ten, its row is already standing in the place its time
won — with the times it beat above it and the ones it pushed down below —
and the name cell of that row is the three slots. There is no confirm to
press: whatever takes you off the card keeps the name. Every way off the
card now stands together in its corner too, with RETIRE beside the way on
rather than across the card from it, under a headline twice the size.

It fits a phone. The board pages to the room the card has, never fewer than
five rows, and always opening on the row the run is on — so the letters
being typed are on screen on a phone held sideways with a keyboard up, not
two pages away.

The stage's own time to beat has moved on the pre-race card as well: on a
laptop or a phone held sideways it stands in the head's far corner, read as
a figure beside the car's other readings instead of as a line of billing
under the stage's name. On a narrow screen it stays where it was.
