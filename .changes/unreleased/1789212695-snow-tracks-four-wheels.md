title: A car now leaves four tyre tracks in snow, lit and treaded, and stops flattening the snow it is riding over
---

What a car wrote into snow was one cross-section swept along the middle of
the car. That is the right shape for exactly one case — a car going where it
is pointing — and on a rally stage that is not most of the time.

**Every wheel now draws its own track.** Driven straight, the rear wheels run
in the tracks their own fronts cut and the car leaves two, as it should.
Sideways, the four wheels sweep four different paths and leave FOUR — the
pair of crossing arcs a drift actually writes into a snowfield. Each band is
laid across the direction that wheel is TRAVELLING rather than the way the
car is pointing, so a tyre being dragged across itself smears a band wider
than its own tread, and the track of a drifting car is wider than the car.
In a caught slide the driver has the front wheels turned into the drift, so
they run far truer than the rears: the fronts cut, the rears scrub, and the
two axles no longer look alike.

**The tread prints.** A rolling tyre stamps its blocks into the snow, one row
per pitch of tread, and a sliding one polishes what it crosses — so the
pattern is drawn as a height field whose gradient bends the surface normal
before the lighting resolves, and it fades out with how sideways the tyre
was going. The blocks are lit by the sun the rest of the world is lit by,
they flatten as it goes down, and they turn to shadow when the car is
between them and the light.

**And the whole thing is lit at all.** The trail was an unlit decal carrying
its own colour, with one flat tint standing in for the time of day: authored
to sit in the snow at noon, it read as a stripe of white paint at dusk and
as a lamp at night, and no tint could tell the sunlit side of a rut from the
shaded one. It is now the same snow as the coat around it — the same lights,
shadows, fog, wrap lighting and glitter, stated once for both surfaces.

**The body stops pressing snow it is riding over.** The broad floor a car's
chassis parts in deep powder was being pressed on every winter stage,
including the snow ROADS, where it put a bladed crown down the middle of
every road in the game — the mark of a vehicle ploughing rather than the one
being driven. A rally car's floor rides a third of a metre over its wheels
and its nose a little under that, so on a road's few centimetres of
already-bladed cover nothing about the body reaches the snow, and what is
left between the tyre tracks is untouched. The trough with a crown down the
middle now appears only where the blanket is deep enough to reach the nose,
which is a drift or a permanent snowfield, and it is the same gate in the
physics and in the picture.
