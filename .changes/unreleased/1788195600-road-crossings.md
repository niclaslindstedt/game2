title: Rally crossings over the public roads
---

Stages now cross the tarmac. Where a public road lies in the rally's way the
route goes straight over it, square — so the two gravel roads meet the seal
exactly opposite each other and the place reads as a crossroads rather than
as two junctions that landed near one another. The road is closed for the
rally on both sides: a barrier stands on each arm, which makes a crossing the
one spot on a stage where two of them face each other across the road you are
on.

And because a public road is built up on a graded formation while a rally
track is scraped along the field beside it, the gravel climbs a short ramp
onto the tarmac and drops off the far edge. Arrive at speed and the car
leaves the ground — over a blind brow, with nothing visible on the far side
until you land.
