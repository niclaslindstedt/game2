title: Gravel thrown sideways off a drift
---

A sliding car now throws a rooster tail of stones out of the side it is
sliding towards — thicker the deeper the slide and the faster the tyres are
being dragged across the road. Stones, not dust: they leave low, arc, and
land, and they are made of whatever ground the car is on.

It comes off the wheels doing the work. The DRIVEN wheels throw more whether
or not the power has broken them loose, and far more once it has, so a
rear-driver sprays off its tail and a front-driver off its nose. And the
OUTER wheels take over as the car goes sideways: the inner ones are running
in the furrow the outer ones just dug, so the tail streams off the outside
of the car rather than out from under the middle of it.
