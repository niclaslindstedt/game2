title: Every slope is now a slope the car can feel
---

A road is a surface, not a line, and the handling now reads it that way.
The curvature that decides a takeoff is the ground's shape along the
direction of TRAVEL — the stage's brows lengthways, and the crown, the bank
and the shoulder across — so riding up the verge and over a road throws the
car with enough speed, where before crossing one was perfectly flat however
fast you did it.

The same number is what the tires are worth on the way there. Crest a rise,
or ride up a bank and run straight off the top, and part of the car's weight
goes into following the ground down: the grip bleeds off with it, the slide
comes easier and a lock that was a corner on the flat is a spin. Push
further and the wheels leave — going light and flying are one continuum now,
not two rules. A compression presses the car on instead, and a level road
still costs an ordinary corner nothing.

And the ground beside the road finally counts as ground: two wheels over the
edge of a sealed road leans the body onto the drop and pulls the car toward
the low side, rather than a place the height fell away while the physics
insisted it was level.
