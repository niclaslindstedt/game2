type: Changed
title: No stage makes you drive straight for more than five seconds
---

Stages are corners joined by straights again, rather than the other way
around. The rule now caps the RUN and not the segment, which is the half that
was missing: a straight was legal on its own, nothing stopped the generator
drawing four of them back to back, and one seed came out with four hundred
metres of nothing in the middle of it. The worst stretch anywhere across
twenty-four seeds was twenty-two seconds; it is now under five, which is the
whole point.

The twenty-two-second one was a public road. Those are laid across the
country before the stage is routed, and they were being drawn as ruled lines
between two points on the map's rim — the bend each one drew for itself was
fed back into its own steering and had decayed to nothing three steps later,
so half the seeds carried a road that ran arrow-straight for over two
kilometres. They wander like country roads now, and where the rally borrows
one it leaves it where the road stops bending instead of sitting on the
straight bit. That also fixes a borrowed stretch quietly drifting off the
tarmac it was supposed to be following: every bend gentler than a rally corner
used to be thrown away and driven as a straight, one chord at a time.

Two things fell out on the way. Two cars that had stopped closing on each
other were never pushed apart — a pair could grind along side by side, most
of a body width inside one another, for as long as neither steered away.
And there is more of everything to drive: more corners, more crests and more
water crossings per stage, a little more sliding, and a stage that takes
about seven percent longer.
