title: Homesteads off the stage
---

The country is lived in. Every so often — far between, never two in sight of
each other — a house stands off the stage: falu red, ochre yellow or white
boards under clay tile, black sheet metal or slate, one storey or two, some
with a porch over the door and some with a wing off the back. A gravel drive
comes down from the yard and meets the rally road square, with a lane of
birches and rowans planted down it and a barrier across its mouth so nobody in
the field is in any doubt which way the stage goes. A car or two stands in the
yard — a hatchback, an estate, a pickup, a van — in the field's own paints or
a plain one.

Every homestead is placed by the generator, so the same seed always has the
same houses, the ground is graded flat under each yard and shelved under each
drive, the forest keeps off both, and the walls, the parked cars and the lane
trees are as solid as they look. The drive is real road: a car that ignores the
tape drives up it on gravel.
