title: Each car carries its weight where it does, and the roll knows it
---

The tall front-driven hatch, the low four-door and the sixties saloon are three different bodies once they are off their wheels: each carries its weight at its own height and its own place along the wheelbase, and every arm the crash works on is measured from there. So the hatch climbs its sill corner on less roll than the four-door and goes over its nose more readily than its tail, a nose-heavy car sliding sideways is spun about its tail first, and the cage every rally car carries now counts against the turn. A car lying flat on its four wheels is no longer nudged by the ground it is resting on.
