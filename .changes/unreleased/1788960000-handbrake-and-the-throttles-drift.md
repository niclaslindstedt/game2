---
type: Changed
title: A handbrake that stops the car, and a rear-driver that holds its drift on the throttle
---

The handbrake is a brake. Locking the rear wheels now costs a third of the
car's braking, and a car sideways on the lever scrubs its speed off far
harder — a full-lock yank slows it right down inside a second and a half.
Its rotation also fades as the car reaches the angle it was asked for,
instead of walking every car in the roster round to a spin. It is the last
resort it was always described as: the way to snap a front-driver round a
hairpin, and something to get off again quickly.

The throttle now separates the three cars. A rear-driver has a real
steady-state drift on the power — full lock at 35° with the pedal down and
27° with it lifted — because the driven rear is what holds the car out
there. A front-driver has no such thing: the throttle pulls it straight, so
it goes the other way, 19° on the power and 24° on a lift. The two want
opposite pedals mid-corner, which is the one thing to relearn moving
between them.

A move — a flick, a trailed brake, the lever — now lifts each layout toward
a ceiling of its OWN rather than toward the rear-driver's. Provoked, the
hatch used to reach exactly the angle the saloon reached, which is why it
was the most sideways car in the game despite being the one that understeers.

How far each car slides on the wheel is unchanged.
