type: Changed
title: The drift slides, instead of steering into the corner
---

Cornering now costs speed the way it should. The tires hold a real ceiling of
lateral grip, so the tightest line a car can carry grows with pace: a long
sweeper is a flat-out drift held at a proper rally angle, and a hairpin has to
be braked for or flicked round on the handbrake instead of pivoted at speed.
The slide itself goes deeper on gravel and hangs out further — the car runs
WIDE while the nose is cocked in, which is what a drift is supposed to do.
