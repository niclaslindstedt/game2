title: The desert's wash trees no longer read as African acacias from a distance
---

A stand of mesquite or ironwood seen from a few hundred metres merged into one
dark band with a flat top — the silhouette of an umbrella-thorn acacia, which
is a tree from the wrong continent.

The trees were open enough close up. The problem was vertical, not horizontal:
every foliage blob sat on the END of a branch, and all six branch tips sat in
one narrow height band, so every leaf the tree owned was in a thin horizontal
plate on top of bare stems. Spreading the leaves sideways cannot fix that, and
the airier the plate got the more it read as a canopy floating over trunks.

The foliage now walks the branch — the same blobs spent between the fork and
the tip instead of piled on the tip — so each tree is a mound with its own
trunks showing through it, and a stand of them has a ragged top.

Which height a tree forks at turned out to be what separates the three species,
so it is now a knob rather than a constant: a mesquite breaks up at a quarter
of its height and is a thicket you cannot see over, an ironwood carries a
rounded crown higher, and a palo verde holds the most bare green stem of the
three. Forked at one height they were the same tree wearing three colours,
which is half of why a bosque merged into a single mass.
