title: Watch the rest of the field come home
---

Crossing the line no longer means the stage is over for you. You still get
the flying finish — the camera planted at the gate, watching you go — but a
few seconds down the run-out it hands over: the crews still out there are
racing for places worth points, and the results card now stands over THAT
instead of over your own car receding. The crew behind you, seen from the
drone, the way the main menu stands over a stage somebody is driving.

Press SPECTATE and you go down to them. The camera flies to the car in a
second flat, in an arc that clears whatever country is in the way, and lands
in the same chase view you raced under. Arrows either side of their name walk
the field one car at a time, each one another flight; when the crew you are
watching crosses the line it cuts to the next one down the road on its own. A
strip along the bottom carries their number, their crew, their clock, where
they stand and how many seconds they are up or down on you at the last split
you have both been through — so a place you are about to lose is something
you watch happen rather than read afterwards.

BACK TO RESULTS puts the card up again at any point and the race carries on
behind it. The classification is exactly the one you would have been handed
had you never looked, and pressing on to the next stage before the last car
is home still records it in full.

HEADS UP is where this happens every time: everybody leaves on one green and
you start on the back row, so crossing the line always leaves a race behind
you. On a rally start it happens when somebody is genuinely still out there —
a crew who went off, or a field whose ten-second head starts were not enough
to cover the difference.
