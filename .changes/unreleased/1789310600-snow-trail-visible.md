title: Snow now visibly gives way under a car, and the trail it leaves is on screen at last
---

A car driving through deep snow left no mark on it. The snow underneath was
really being compressed — it slowed the car, it held differently once packed,
and a second pass down the same line really was faster — but nothing about
the picture said so, and a stage full of driven snow drew back as smooth
white ground.

Two things were wrong, and they hid each other.

The trail itself was being drawn, correctly and completely, with every
triangle facing INTO the ground: the section runs across the car and the
stamps run along it, and in this world's handedness those two axes cross
downward, so a front-face material threw the whole mesh away every frame.
A trail laid, shaped and coloured, and never once on screen.

And the coat of snow the country wears — a mesh that is supposed to BEND
where a car has been — was sinking behind the ground tiles under it, which
were still drawn at the height the snow stood at before anybody touched it.
The tiles are now laid a car's belly-clearance under the untouched top, which
is exactly the room the coat needs to be pressed into, and nothing that lies
on the coat may sink past it either.

What a car leaves behind now is the shape of the car: a broad trough at about
its own clearance, where the body pressed the snow down to get where it is,
with two furrows cut into the floor of it under the wheels and the crown it
straddled standing between them. It is not a snowplough's lane — the walls of
thrown snow the trail used to try to stand up either side of itself are gone,
because the ground itself now does the work they were standing in for.
