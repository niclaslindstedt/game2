title: A car sliding on its shell can be tipped back over
---

A car that has gone over and come to rest on its roof or its flank is still
in the accident: slide it over the lip of a bank or a ditch and it goes over
again, instead of grinding along as though the ground were a table. The roll
now reads the shape of what it is lying on rather than assuming a level
floor, so a body on a hillside settles the way the hillside holds it.

The two halves of an accident also have names now — the car turning over its
corners, and the car lying flat on a face still travelling — so what you
watch and hear can tell the difference between a cartwheel and a long grind
on the roof.
