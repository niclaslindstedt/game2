title: Lakes, swamps, and a gravel road that is not a plane
---

The country a stage is set in now has hollows in it that hold water, in
three sizes: small deep pools, proper tarns, and broad shallow meres. The
shallow ones are SWAMPS — you can see the bottom, reeds and bulrushes stand
in them, and willows and alders lean out over the edges. Drowned trunks
still standing in the water, sphagnum, tussocks and lily pads finish them
off, and the bog now grows where the ground is actually wet rather than
wherever a noise field happened to put one.

And a gravel road is no longer a perfect ribbon. It has bumps in it here and
there — a frost heave, a hollow worn at a corner exit — each of them
marginal, with clean road between them. Its width wanders by about a tenth
either way over hundreds of metres, the way a bladed road does. Tarmac gets
none of it: a paving machine lays a plane at a constant width, and the
sealed sections are meant to be the smooth part.
