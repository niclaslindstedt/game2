title: Snow on everything standing, and rivers that freeze
---

A winter stage went white on the ground and nowhere else: black trees, bare
boulders and dry roofs standing on a snowfield. Every tree, bush, stone,
roof, fence rail, sleeper and parked car now carries the load, on the faces
that would actually hold it and fading in over the same metres the ground
whitens through — and it costs nothing, because it is a term in the
materials the world already draws rather than a second set of snowed models.

The country's hillsides are covered too. The face rule that keeps snow off
a slope is the summer alpine's — the wind-scoured edge of the permanent
snow — and it was leaving a winter as white flats between brown hills; deep
in the cold it now relaxes until only near-vertical rock is bare.

And the rivers freeze. Standing water has gone over at -5°C since the ice
arrived; moving water asks for far more, because the current keeps feeding
heat back to the surface and a fast reach never closes at all. So a river
under a hard winter is white down its flats and pools and black at its
drops, its ford still running, and the reaches that have closed are floors
the car drives across.
