title: Snow on everything standing, a ploughed trail, and rivers that freeze
---

A winter stage went white on the ground and nowhere else: black trees, bare
boulders and dry roofs standing on a snowfield. Every tree, bush, stone,
roof, fence rail, sleeper and parked car now carries the load, on the faces
that would actually hold it and breaking into clumps with the dark showing
through rather than fading out as a white wash — and it costs nothing,
because it is a term in the materials the world already draws rather than a
second set of snowed models. Two of the same tree standing side by side wear
different snow.

The country's hillsides are covered too. The face rule that keeps snow off a
slope is the summer alpine's — the wind-scoured edge of the permanent snow —
and it was leaving a winter as white flats between brown hills; deep in the
cold it now relaxes until only near-vertical rock is bare. And the whole
country is brighter, because snow bounces most of the light that falls on it
back up: a snowfield now lifts and cools the light it is lit by, which is
what makes one bright under a sun too low to light anything else.

A car driving through it ploughs a real trail: each tyre's rut with its
floor pressed flat, the snow it displaced standing in a broken bank outside
it, and the strip between the wheels left standing as a crown — as proud as
the snow where the car is has to throw, so a packed snow road leaves the same
trail drawn flat.

And the rivers freeze. Standing water has gone over at -5°C since the ice
arrived; moving water asks for far more, because the current keeps feeding
heat back to the surface and a fast reach never closes at all. So a river
under a hard winter is white down its flats and pools and black at its drops,
its ford still running, and the reaches that have closed are floors the car
drives across.
