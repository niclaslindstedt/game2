title: A railway across the country, crossed on a ramp, with a train on it
---

The taiga has a railway now. It is laid across the map before the rally is
routed, the way the public roads are — a single track at a railway's radii,
sweeping round the lakes, kept off the tarmac — and the stage plans round it
as it does round a road. It may never borrow or join it; where it has to get
past, it crosses SQUARE on a ramp the organisers built: three metres of lip a
few metres short of the rails, with the road at grade and the rails laid flush
in a planked deck on the far side. Taken at pace, the car flies over the
line. Taken slowly, it lands on the rails.

Which matters, because there is a train. Every crossing has its own
timetable: one comes through around the time a driver at stage pace reaches
it and another every couple of minutes after, each way in turn — a diesel
hauling timber, box or tank wagons, or the cream-and-red railbus on its own.
The train is as solid as it looks and moving as fast as it looks: a car on
the rails is hit by it, a car in the air over a wagon's roof is not. Both arms
of the line run to the edge of the map on a ballast bed with sleepers and
rails, and the crossing is signed with a crossed board on each approach. The
level map lists every crossing (`RX1`) with its timetable, and the analyzer's
`jumps.train` check asks whether the ramp clears a wagon at stage pace.
