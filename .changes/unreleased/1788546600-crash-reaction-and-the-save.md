title: A crossed-up landing is yours to save, and the ground now changes the crash
---

Two things a rollover was missing.

**The landing you can still save.** A car that comes down sideways used to
trip by a fixed amount — the driver's hands and feet reached it not at all.
Now three things set how hard the tyres bite, and all of them are committed
in the air: which way the wheel is pointed (aim the fronts along the travel
and the front axle stops tripping the car; saw the other way and it trips
harder — and you can over-counter, because the best catch points them exactly
along the slide), whether a pedal is already spending the tyres' budget
(a brake or a boot roughly halves the bite, and costs you the sideways speed
you would rather have scrubbed), and how hard the car arrived (a slam loads
the tyres past their own weight and bites half again as hard, so getting flat
and level over the lip is the other half of the save). On the landing that
sits right on the line, caught-and-braked is the only input that stays on its
wheels.

**And the corner it lands on turns it.** The ground could previously do one
thing to a crash: take speed out of it. Every accident then ran out in the
plane it started in, and which part of the car hit first changed nothing. The
ground now pushes back where it is actually touching, so a fall onto a nose
corner pitches the car out of the barrel roll it was in — the change of hand
half way through an accident that a rollover is famous for. It saturates as
the shell folds, so a hard arrival deforms the car instead of stopping the
roll dead.
