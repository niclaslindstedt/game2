title: Blowdowns, thickets and rocky outcrops
---

The taiga has grown up. Trees now stand in tight knots of three or four with real light between them rather than one every ten metres, each mature trunk keeping a skirt of saplings, junipers and moss around its foot. Gales have been through the deep wild: patches of old timber lie down the fall line in parallel, root plates on end, with dead standing stems and snapped trunks around them. Loose rock has stopped being loose — stone now breaks surface as outcrops of five to ten boulders bedded into a hillside along its contour, and half the stone in the country has gone over to moss on the faces that look at the sky.
