title: A car parts the snow with its nose instead of sinking into it
---

The snow a car drives through was only pressed down where the four wheels
went, and only after they had been over it — so a car in deep snow sat on top
of undisturbed snow and left a trail appearing behind it, when what has to
happen is the opposite: the snow under the body comes down FIRST, or the car
could not be there at all.

The body now parts the snow along the line its nose sweeps, ahead of the
wheels, and the wheels cut their own deeper ruts into the floor that leaves.
A track through deep snow is now a broad trough the width of the car with two
furrows in it and untouched snow standing beyond the flanks, which is what one
actually looks like.

...and what the car is ploughing is measured at the nose, off the snow it is
about to meet, rather than under wheels standing on ground the body has
already flattened — otherwise a car softened its own path and deep snow got
easier the harder it was driven into.
