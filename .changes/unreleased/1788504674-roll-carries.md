title: A rollover carries its momentum instead of stopping dead
---

Going over used to look like the car had hit glue: the roll started, the
speed fell to nothing inside a second or two, and the car spun on the spot
until the crew were sent back to the last split board. It now does what a
rolling car does — bounces forward, covers real ground, and smashes into
whatever is in its way, which sends it over again in a new direction.

Three things were taking the speed. A flat scrub on the travel beside the
ground's own friction; contacts charged for arrivals the body never made
(the centre climbing its own corner under the roll, and the tyres touching
down every time the roll passed through upright); and, on top of that, a
rolling car rode straight over every stone and post in its path instead of
hitting it. A car that goes over at 165 km/h now travels the better part of
a hundred metres, and takes rather less damage doing it, because a roll no
longer pays for a dozen impacts that never happened.
