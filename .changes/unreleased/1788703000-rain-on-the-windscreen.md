title: Rain on the windscreen, and wipers that clear it
---

Drive a wet stage from the COCKPIT and the glass in front of you is now
weather rather than a clean pane: water beads on it, the beads that break
loose run and leave dotted trails behind them, and a continuous film of
water ripples under the lot. Everything behind a drop is BENT through it —
a bead sitting on the grass shows you a piece of the horizon — so the road
you are reading is genuinely harder to read, which is what driving in rain
actually costs.

It runs the right way, too. Standing on the line the water creeps DOWN the
screen; at rally pace the air coming over the scuttle drags it UP, drawn
out into tears, and a corner leans the runs toward the outside of the bend.

And the wiper now means something. The arm clears exactly the arc it
sweeps, the screen beads up again behind it — in a downpour, before the
stroke has finished — the corners it cannot reach stay streaming for the
whole stage, and it leaves the faint arcs of smear on the glass that rubber
leaves, gone within the second.
