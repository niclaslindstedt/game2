title: The trail a car ploughs through snow reads again
---

Driving through deep snow leaves a trail the coat itself sags into. The coat
was sampling how far the wheels had packed the snow down at a single point
every two and a half metres — but a wheel rut is three quarters of a metre
wide and the pair of them a metre and a half apart, so a vertex either landed
in a rut or missed it and the snow dimpled at random instead of sagging. It
now reads the whole cell, so the coat carries the broad hollow a driven-over
patch of snow has while the wheel tracks themselves are drawn at the size they
actually are.

The coat was also redrawing all six thousand of its vertices every frame a car
was carving snow, which is most of them; it now redraws only the ground around
the car, where the snow can actually have changed.
