title: The distant mountains sit in the light now
---

The ridge rings on the horizon were painted a flat mid-grey whatever the
hour, so at night they hung in front of the sky as a chalky chain instead
of standing in it as silhouettes — worst under The Long Dark's midnight
storm, where the sky is black and the mountains were twenty-five times
brighter than the ceiling behind them. Each vertex is now shaded against
the sky at its own height, so a range dissolves into the lit gap under a
cloud base at its feet and goes to soot at its summits, and the snow on
the far peaks takes the colour of the light on it — warm at noon, orange
in a sunset, a dim blue under the moon, grey under a storm — rather than
washing out to chalk.

The whole night sky is darker with it: the weather's grey lid and the
season's own cast over the air are colours daylight makes, and both now
fade out as the sun goes down instead of greying a midnight sky that has
a black ceiling drawn over it.
