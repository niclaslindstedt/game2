title: A crash distorts the car instead of bouncing it — the wall, the shell and the ground all give
---

What comes back off a wall, a bank or another car now falls with how hard it was hit: a bumper springs back from a nudge, and a car that meets a rooted trunk at 120 km/h is stopped and folded where it stands instead of thrown back up the road at 35. The shell is no longer one material — the nose and tail fold as crumple zones, a flank as a door over its bars, the roof as the cage, which folds a hand's breadth and no further and throws the body by what it does not fold — and every face hardens toward the bare cage as it is used up, so a rollover's fifth contact kicks the car where its first one stopped it. A heavier car is turned less by the same corner arriving and folds deeper for it. And the ground under a crash is no longer steel: gravel, soil and sand each take a share of an arrival as their own furrow, which turns the body less and marks the shell less than the same fall onto tarmac, and a sill dragging a furrow through loose ground costs friction a door skin on pavement does not. The health schematic reads the roof against the cage's own stroke.
