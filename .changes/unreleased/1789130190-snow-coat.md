type: Fixed
title: Snow is a coat on the ground, not white paint on it
---

A winter stage's snow now lies ON the country instead of tinting it. The
blanket used to be sampled on the ground's own fourteen-metre lattice, which
is far too coarse to hold a snowfield's edge — the bank at a ploughed road's
lip rises over four metres, fell between two corners and was erased — so the
snow arrived as flat white ground with no thickness anywhere in it.

It is now sampled on a grid of its own and drawn as its own surface: a coat
with real depth, a hard bank standing along the road, and a shaded face where
a drift falls away. A permanent alpine snowfield is metres deep rather than
the half metre a summer's air temperature used to allow, so deep snow finally
slows a car down the way it should — and anything shorter than the snow over
it (a stone, a stump, a fallen limb) is buried, neither hit nor drawn, so
leaving the road into open white ground no longer destroys the car on
something invisible under the surface.

The snow has a shader of its own as well — light scattering back out of the
pack instead of a hard terminator, and sunlight glinting off the crystals.
And the spectator paths crossing a snowfield are trodden snow now, a trough
between two banks, rather than a strip of bare mud laid across the white.
