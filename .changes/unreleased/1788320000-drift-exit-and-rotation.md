title: The drift's exit, and three ways of rotating the car
---

Letting go of the wheel mid-slide no longer gathers the car up for free. The
tires used to eat the whole sideways momentum on their own, so a drift
finished the corner by itself and handed the car back straight, on the line
and faster than it went in. Now the car carries on out toward the outside of
the road, aimed off the line, and steering is what tips it back into the
middle — the catch is the driver's again.

The pedals rotate the car too. Lifting off mid-corner takes the drift DEEPER
as well as tightening the line, and booting it again brings the tail round on
a car with driven rear wheels — a stab, not a state, so it spikes and settles.
A front-driver still answers the same pedal by pulling itself straight.

The handbrake now pulls the tightest arc the car has: the lever unsticks the
rear without taking away the front wheels' ability to steer, so it is finally
the tool for a hairpin rather than a very sideways way of running wide.
