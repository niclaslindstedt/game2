title: White rain skies, black thunderstorms, and thunder that arrives late
---

Rain and thunderstorms are weather rather than a grey filter. Every wet sky now
has a cloud DECK over it: rain is a white one, bright enough overhead to be the
brightest thing on the stage and greying toward the horizon; a storm is a black
one with the only daylight left in the strip that gets in under its base. The
fair-weather clouds drop below the base and become scud, flattened and tearing
along under the ceiling. How heavy a stage's weather is comes from its own
seeded wind, so no two wet stages get the same sky — and under a black one the
car turns its lights on at noon.

The rain itself falls at the speed it is SEEN at, so driving into it turns the
sheet into streaks coming at the windscreen instead of a shower travelling
along with the car; drops vary in size, thicken with each squall, and are drawn
dark against a bright sky and pale against a black one.

Lightning strikes in cells rather than on a timer, forks a real channel to the
ground when it is close and lights the cloud from inside when it is not,
flickers through several return strokes, and lights the world from its own
bearing. Thunder arrives afterwards at the speed of sound — a crack up close, a
long roll from kilometres out — and a storm you have stopped in still has the
gale in it.
