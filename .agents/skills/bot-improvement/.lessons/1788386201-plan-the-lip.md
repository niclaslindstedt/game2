title: The plan must take the flight and the landing out of the braking distance, and the lever is not an answer at ninety over

With a physics model that punishes over-rotation, three bot habits the old
arcade slide had been hiding turned into respawns (seed 5, both rear-drivers;
seed 3 classic):

- **A hairpin 40 m past a lip.** The plan's `allowed = √(cap² + braking·d)`
  counted the flight as braking road; the bot took the lip at 142 km/h,
  landed 12 m from the corner and had only the slide. Past the nearest
  `samples[i].jump` in the horizon the braking room is the road up to the
  lip plus what is left after `LIP_GLIDE` (1.5 s: ~1.1 s of air plus the
  landing skitter). The corner scan skips straights, so the lip needs its
  own plain walk.
- **The lever at any overspeed.** `LEVER_HOT` (12 m/s over the corner cap)
  is what a slide can scrub; over it, brake first and lever later.
- **Flicking over a brow.** `browAhead` reads the road's vertical curvature
  over `air.crestSpan` for `BROW_LOOK` seconds and weighs the pull against
  `air.hold`; it gates `nearHard` and adds to the counter-steer weight.

Replay one seed per suspect with a probe that logs the events with speed,
slip, steer, `provoked`, `chain` and `settle` — the sim table only says
which run went wrong, the log says which move.
