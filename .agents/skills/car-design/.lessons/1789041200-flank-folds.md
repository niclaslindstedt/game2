---
title: Anything laid ALONG the flank must sample the body's own folds — and the wheel arch is a fold with a STEP in it
date: 2026-08-27
scope: pwa/src/game/car/shell.ts, pwa/src/game/car/trim.ts, pwa/src/game/car/fascia.ts
concepts: [livery, decals, stripes, flares, wheel-arch, bumper]
---

A stripe, a rubbing strip, a race number or a bumper wrap is drawn a few mm
proud of `flankX(z, y)` — a curve. Step it on a uniform ladder and each
straight span becomes a chord UNDER that curve, so the bodywork comes
through the paint as vertical slivers that read as drips, or through a
bumper wrap as a row of triangular teeth. No amount of `proud` fixes it: the
overshoot is half a box flare's whole extra width.

`bandSamples` in `shell.ts` samples at its own ladder UNION the flare
stations and the profile stations for exactly this reason; `sideBand` is
built on it and a new builder must reuse it or do the same. What neither
names is the WHEEL ARCH, and `archAt` STEPS from `floorY` to
`wheelRadius + lift` at `axle ± arches.radius`, with the steepest part of
its curve just inside that. Anything reaching the arch needs
`axle ± radius·{1, 0.9, 0.6}` in its stations too; with those, a four-rung
ladder keeps every bumper on every car clear of its own bodywork.

Two more, both of which look like rendering bugs rather than sampling bugs.
**Per-cell depth splits a glyph**: sampling `flankX` separately for each cell
of a blocky number puts neighbouring cells at slightly different x and the
glyph shows hairline cracks at a grazing angle — compute ONE plane (the max
`flankX` over its footprint) and lay every cell on it. And a band must never
hang inside a wheel opening: `sideBand` takes `overArch` — `clip` lets the
arch eat into a painted panel, `ride` keeps a rocker stripe's height and
arcs it over. Both cap at just above the belt; without that cap a band
pushed up by an arch climbs onto the shoulder and balloons into a dome.

Measure it rather than eyeball it: a dozen lines of `tsx` over `flankX` that
walks each span and prints `flankX(z) − chord(z)` in millimetres says in one
run what a contact sheet only hints at.
